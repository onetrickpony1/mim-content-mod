# Lizz (barracuda): night log

Choices made overnight for [[2026-11-18 Night job - the rest of the fish, Claude decides]]. Summary in [[Night fish log 2026-11-19]]. † = not checked.

## Species and name
- **Species:** European barracuda, *Sphyraena sphyraena*, **Lizz** in the species report.

## Size
- **Size in game:** 1.5 blocks long (24 px), body box 0.7 x 0.3. Long and slim, about as long as a young tonn is wide-ish; shorter than the cerna (1.81).
- **Why:** the report says it reaches 1.2 m; most are about half that†. 1.5 blocks reads as a big, slim hunter without towering over the cerna.

## Behaviour, spawns, rarity
- **Behaviour:** schools of up to 4 (the report: young ones shoal, adults are alone or in pairs). Health 8 (4 hearts). Harmless for now (no bite).
- **Spawns:** near the coast, `bahar_sea`, `dwejra_bahar_sea`, `qawra_inland_sea`, weight 2, groups of 2 to 4. Vanilla: warm and lukewarm oceans, weight 1.
- **Rarity:** uncommon. The content list calls it a summer fish near the coast; the mod has no seasons yet, so it is simply rarer all year. A summer-only spawn can come with the calendar.
- **Stings:** no.
- **Drops:** itself (1 raw fish, `mimcontent:lizz`). Bucket, spawn egg. It is shark bait.

## Art (night job, made in order: model, icon, bucket)
Photos used (reference only, free licences, list in `art/references/fish_lizz_refonly/list.tsv`): a fresh fish on a plate and one on a board (for true colour out of water), the d'Orbigny plate, and divers' photos of schools. The spawn egg is still the placeholder (not asked for tonight).

**3D model: passed in 2 critic rounds**
- Picked candidate C of three (A slim, B deeper, C slim with a longer head and visible bars), because the long head and bars read most like a barracuda.
- 1.5 blocks long (24 px), 3 px deep, 2 px wide: a long, slim torpedo. The first build came out 29 px, as long as the cerna, so it was shortened to the 1.5 blocks in the size note above.
- Long pointed head, the lower jaw sticking out one pixel past the upper snout. No teeth.
- Two small dorsal fins far apart, one mid-back and one far back; small grey fins below; a dark forked tail that sways.
- Colours from the fresh-fish photos, one step lighter for the water: dark slate back, silver sides, white belly (dark back, pale belly), faint dark bars on the upper flank.
- One black eye with pale grey round it, so it doesn't sink into the dark back under water.
- Round 1: PASS, with polish (a flatter back, smaller fins, a clearer eye, straight bars, the pelvic fin not like a peg). Applied, then round 2: PASS.
- Left as suggestions from round 2 (not done): thicker tail lobes, an even smaller front dorsal, one more pixel of jaw.
- Size check: in the tank beside the cerna, a small (2 block) tonn and a cod, it is longer and much slimmer than the cod and shorter than the cerna and tonn.

**Item icon: passed in 2 critic rounds**
- Round 1 (three thin, corner-to-corner candidates): FAIL, "a syringe or sword blade". Blind readers said: "Raw fish (small tan/brown fish)" (they had miscounted and named the cod; of our icon they said "a needlefish, garfish or sardine-type"), "Pufferfish (an orange, spiky, round fish with blue fins)" (miscounted too; they called our icon "a sword or a thin blade"), "Raw fishing line or thread (a coiled white string)" (then corrected to "Fishing rod").
- Round 2: redrawn as a spindle (a little wider in the middle), lit on the back from the top left, a dark grey outline along the belly side so no white touches the edge, two separate dorsal fins and a real forked tail. Picked C of three (C has the fullest, fan-shaped fork; A and B's tails looked like a sword's crossguard). Critic: PASS, narrowly.
- Blind readers, round 2, first lines word for word:
  - "slot 3: a slender silver fish, probably a mackerel or garfish type"
  - "slot 2: a long, thin, silvery blue fish (needlefish or garfish type, with a pointed beak-like snout)"
  - "slot 5: a long, thin silver-blue fish with a pointed beak, like a garfish or needlefish (a barracuda is less likely)"
- All three now see a long, thin fish, but none says "barracuda". The owner may want another pass.
- Left as suggestions (not done): short dark bars across the white stripe, the white moved toward the belly, the loose fin pixels joined to the outline.
- Colours: the model's slate and grey tones lifted about 1.2 times for out of water; the silvers stay near white.

**Bucket: passed in 2 critic rounds**
- Only the long head and the front of the body come out of the water, the jaw poking just past the bucket's left edge; no tail. The back of the rim stays visible.
- Picked candidate A (the head level) over B (the head tilted up, which made the snout too short).
- Round 1: FAIL. The dark back didn't read, and the pale crown made a knob. Fixed: a dark slate crown and back, a silver jaw point with no dark mouth line, and a lighter rim against the water. Round 2: PASS.
- Blind readers (round 1 version), first lines word for word:
  - "slot 3: bucket of water with a silvery blue-grey fish in it (pointed snout, pale belly; it may be a needlefish or garfish, but I'm not sure)"
  - "slot 2: a water bucket holding a slim, pale silvery-blue fish with its head pointing up-left (it looks like a garfish/needlefish or a sardine-type fish; I can't name the species for sure)"
  - "slot 5: bucket with a slender silvery blue-grey fish in it, pointed snout and black eye, lying on a diagonal (looks like a needlefish or garfish, or some other thin silver fish)"
- Left as suggestions (not done): a whiter belly edge and a softer centre stripe, an even darker back so it stands further from the anchovy bucket, a tapered head top.

**Bucket redo (2026-11-19, after the owner's review)**
- The owner: "barracuda bucket fails looks like a duck or seehorse with long nose" ([[2026-11-19 Lizz bucket redo]]). The upright head on a thin neck read as a bird's bill.
- New pose: the whole long body lies across the bucket mouth at a low slant, the head at the upper left just over the rim and the body running down to the right into the water. No neck.
- Candidates: A (full-depth body, one dorsal fin), B (the forked tail breaking the water at the right), C (slimmer, both dorsals). Installed: B.
- Round 1 critic: PASS (conditional), B best; "None of the three reads as one now" (a bird, duck or seahorse). Fixes applied: a darker continuous back line, two separate tail prongs, bars, a grey jaw tip.
- Round 2 critic: PASS (conditional); the tall tail column could look like a resting gull. Fixed: a short "<" fork, one straight dark back, darker bars, less white.
- Round 3 critic: PASS; "Bird / duck / seahorse read: No." Not applied (for the owner to decide): sink the pale back half under the water so it reads slimmer, a lighter pixel above the eye, slimmer tail lobes.
- Blind readers, round 3 (installed version), first lines word for word:
  - "slot 3: a bucket of water with a pale silver-white fish in it" (bird, duck or seahorse: "No"; it could not make out the eye)
  - "slot 5: bucket of a pale silver-grey fish (a silvery fish like a bream or mullet)" (bird, duck or seahorse: "It does not look like a bird, a duck or a seahorse")
  - "slot 2: water bucket holding a pale silvery-white fish (looks like a silver sea fish such as a bream or mullet type, not a specific vanilla fish)" (bird, duck or seahorse: "No, not really"; the raised tail could "just hint at a duck's tail")
- Earlier redo rounds' readers all said a fish, none a bird or seahorse; two said the head end could pass for a small bird's head "for a moment".
- Nobody named it a barracuda: they called it a silver fish, bream, mullet or mackerel.

## Bucket, option 2 (rebuilt from the accepted icon)
