# Jellyfish

Jellyfish are part of every Maltese summer. "Hemm il-brim?" (are there jellyfish?) is the first question at a bay ([[Maltese life ideas]], idea 65). This note holds the research and the ideas for making them a hazard in the game ([[2026-11-18 Jellyfish - research and hazard ideas]]). It is [[Content mod list]] no. 80. **Ideas only: nothing is built until the owner picks.**

## The short version
- Malta has over 40 kinds of jellyfish and their relatives. Seven matter for the game.
- **The mauve stinger** is the common one. It stings hard, comes in swarms that the wind pushes into bays, and glows when touched.
- **The fried-egg jellyfish** is big, pretty and harmless. It comes in late summer.
- **The Portuguese man o' war** is rare and the most dangerous. It floats on top and stings even when dead on the sand.
- Maltese first aid is **sea water, baking soda, a card to scrape, then ice**. Not fresh water, and in Malta's own advice not vinegar either.
- A **purple flag** on a Maltese beach means jellyfish.

Also recorded, not proposed for the game: the compass jellyfish (*Chrysaora*) and the Australian spotted jellyfish (*Phyllorhiza punctata*, harmless, alien) [1][18]; the stinging siphonophore *Forskalia edwardsii* (Għar Lapsi, March 2021; "extremely painful") [12][20]; comb jellies, which don't sting and some glow [1][3].

**Maltese names.** *Brama* is the general word for a jellyfish [2]. The plural *bram* is from the Maltese Sign Language dictionary [21]. The phrase "Hemm il-brim?" in [[Maltese life ideas]] uses another plural form, *brim* †: no source found for it, so check with a Maltese speaker. "Brama Komuni" (common jellyfish) appears in MaltaToday, but without a species [22]. None of the seven species has a sourced Maltese name of its own.

**A correction to the list.** [[Content mod list]] no. 80 says "purple glow at night". The mauve stinger's bell is mauve, but the light it gives off when it is touched or moved by waves is **green** [24]. It rises to the surface at night and sinks by day [24].

## Seasons and causes

### Month by month (Malta)
| Months | What comes |
|---|---|
| January to March | Mature mauve stingers arrive, spawn and die. By-the-wind sailors from February [1][2] |
| March to May | Portuguese man o' war season (rare) [7]. Crystal jellyfish [13]. Young mauve stingers from mid-spring [1] |
| May to June | The mauve stinger swarm "right before summer" [4]; Golden Bay, May 2022 [25] |
| July to August | Mauve stingers can stay (Pretty Bay, August 2018) [17] |
| August to November | Fried-egg jellyfish [1][16] |

### Bloom years
- Until 1998 the mauve stinger had a big outbreak about **every 12 years**, and each lasted about **4 years**. Since 1999 outbreaks come more often [1].
- One study followed it from 1980 to 1986. Coastal swarms began in 1980, were densest in 1981 to 1983, then got shorter and thinner, mostly in March and April [26].
- After a **dry, calm winter** the mauve stinger comes earlier and in greater numbers [2]. A long dry, warm January in 2018 led to an early, strong bloom [4].

### Wind decides the bay
- Swarms near the coast are **carried there passively**, and the wind direction mostly decides where they end up [26].
- **Xlokk (south-east) winds** have left thousands of mauve stingers on the south-east and east coasts of Malta and Gozo, for example Pretty Bay in January 2020 [1][3].
- Strong winds in April 2023 washed thousands of by-the-wind sailors ashore, mostly on the east coast (Baħar iċ-Ċagħaq, Sliema, Marsaskala) and at Golden Bay, Xwejni, Xlendi and Dwejra [14].
- A local weather page made a daily list of "jellyfish safe" bays from wind, waves, swell and currents [27]. So locals really pick their beach by wind and jellyfish.
- The bays exposed to the **Majjistral (north-west)** are Għajn Tuffieħa, Ramla tal-Mixquqa (Golden Bay), Ġnejna, Fomm ir-Riħ, St Paul's Bay, Il-Prajjet, Paradise Bay, Armier, Comino, Marsalforn, Dwejra and Xlendi [28]. That list is about waves, so using it for jellyfish is our guess (it follows from the wind rule above) †.

### Why there are more now
- Warmer seas, runoff from land, **overfishing**, and hard structures such as harbour walls and offshore platforms [2].
- The nomad jellyfish reaching Malta is put down to the warming sea [10].

### Time of day
- The mauve stinger rises to the surface at night and goes deeper by day [24]. So night swimmers meet more of them.

## Stings and remedies

### What Malta advises
The Malta Red Cross (via the rangers) and the Spot the Jellyfish team give the same steps [19][25][4]:
1. Get out and **rinse with sea water**. Don't rub.
2. Put on a paste of **half baking soda, half sea water** for a few minutes.
3. **Scrape off** what is left of the tentacles with a plastic card, in one direction.
4. **Ice** wrapped in a cloth, for 5 to 15 minutes.
5. **Not** fresh water, vinegar, alcohol, ammonia or a bandage. Get help at once if breathing gets hard.

- Rangers on Maltese beaches carry **baking soda (bicarbonate)** in their first aid kits for stings [29].
- **Vinegar is argued about.** A 2012 MaltaToday guide still said vinegar or alcohol after the rinse [8]. The newer Maltese advice says no vinegar [19][25]. Spot the Jellyfish says treatment depends on the species, and vinegar is only one option [2].
- **The Portuguese man o' war is different:** its venom breaks down with heat, so it gets **hot packs** [6], or hot water at 40 to 45 °C for 20 to 45 minutes, with cold packs only if there is no hot water [30]. Get out of the water at once when one is seen [6].
- **Dead jellyfish still sting.** "Even a lifeless jellyfish has the capability to sting" [8]. The man o' war is very painful even when dead [9].
- **Idea 65 in [[Maltese life ideas]] says "vinegar or a fresh-water rinse cures it".** The research says the opposite: fresh water makes it worse. That line should change if the owner agrees (question 5).

### Flags, nets and other measures
- **The purple flag.** In Malta's beach flag system, purple means stinging jellyfish or other pests are present [31][28]. A purple flag went up at Golden Bay in June 2022 [19].
- **Rangers** patrolled Golden Bay and warned bathers during a 2022 swarm [25].
- **Anti-jellyfish nets.** In 2014 and 2015 the MED-JELLYRISK project put up 15 nets in Italy, Spain, Tunisia and Malta. They came in **25 m long sections** and ran from the shore down to **2.5 m deep**, on sand and rock [32]. Malta's net was at **Pretty Bay** [11]. No source found for nets at Għajn Tuffieħa, Golden Bay, St George's Bay or Balluta †.
- **A forecast.** In 2015 the University of Malta opened a free online model: say where you saw a swarm, and it maps where it will drift over the next four days and which shores it will hit [11].
- **Spot the Jellyfish.** A University of Malta campaign since June 2010, the longest-running national one in the Mediterranean [1]. In ten years people sent over 3,000 reports, about one a day; five or ten a day in summer, none on stormy days [2]. You match what you saw to a picture guide, note the date, time and number, and send it in [1]. Seaside panels were paid for by the Malta Tourism Authority [1].

### Who eats them
- The **loggerhead turtle** (*fekruna komuni*) is the turtle seen most in Maltese waters, mainly in spring and from August to November. Sea turtles eat fish, jellyfish, crustaceans and squid, and sometimes plastic bags they take for jellyfish [33]. A young loggerhead in Malta died after eating a balloon, probably taken for a jellyfish [34]. Loggerheads have nested at Golden Bay and Ġnejna [37][38].

## How other games do it (for contrast only)
- **Vanilla Minecraft has no jellyfish.** Players keep asking for them on the feedback site [35].
- **Upgrade Aquatic** (a Forge mod) has three: a passive-aggressive cassiopea, an "immortal" one that heals, and an aggressive, poisonous box jellyfish in warm oceans [36].
- Most jellyfish mods make a jellyfish a mob that poisons on touch and spawns all year. None that we found ties them to wind, season or a real place. That is where this mod can be different.

## Hazard ideas

Size: **small** (a day or two of work), **medium** (a few days), **big** (a week or more, or new systems). "Calendar" means it needs the mod's calendar ([[2026-11-17 Calendar both, start with phase 1 and its art]]: an in-game year with a length you can set, and an option to follow the real date), which **isn't built yet**. "Wind" means it needs a wind of the day (idea 51 in [[Maltese life ideas]]), which isn't built either.

### A. Seasonal
1. **Each kind in its own months.** *You see:* winter mauve stingers, spring sailors and man o' war, the late-summer fried-eggs. *How:* each species has a spawn chance per month (the table above). Out of season it is rare or absent. *Why Maltese:* the season table above [1][2][7][16]. *Size:* small once the calendar exists. *Needs:* calendar.
2. **Bloom years.** *You see:* some years the sea is thick with mauve stingers, other years hardly any. *How:* each in-game (or real) year is quiet or a bloom year. A bloom lasts a few years in a row, then quiet years follow. Since real outbreaks have come more often since 1999 [1], bloom years could be the usual case and quiet years the treat. *Why Maltese:* the 12-year cycle and its change [1][26]. *Size:* small. *Needs:* calendar.
3. **A calm winter means an early, big bloom.** *You see:* after a dry winter, the swarm comes weeks early. *How:* the game counts rainy days in winter. Few rainy days: the swarm starts earlier and is bigger. *Why Maltese:* [2][4]. *Size:* small. *Needs:* calendar.
4. **The sailors wash up in spring.** *You see:* thousands of little blue rafts on the sand after a windy spring day. A day later they turn pale, look like plastic, and smell. *How:* a thin "beached sailors" layer on sand blocks after a strong spring wind. It fades in a few days. A tiny sting (a short itch) if you walk on it barefoot. *Why Maltese:* [14][9]. *Size:* medium. *Needs:* calendar; wind helps.

### B. Real-timed
5. **Follow the real date.** *You see:* in real April you meet a man o' war, in real August the fried-eggs. *How:* the calendar's real-date option reads the computer's own clock. **This works fully offline.** *Why Maltese:* same seasons as the real island. *Size:* small, part of the calendar. *Needs:* calendar.
6. **Live jellyfish news (optional, off by default).** *You see:* if a real swarm is at Golden Bay today, the game's Golden Bay gets one too. *How:* there is no public Spot the Jellyfish data feed that we found. The honest way would be a small daily file on the owner's own site (maltaminecraft.com) that the owner or a helper updates by hand from the news. The mod would fetch only that file, send no personal details (AGENTS.md rule 6), and fall back to ideas 1 to 3 when offline. A live wind feed from a weather service is possible too, but that is another outside service, so it needs the owner's OK. *Why Maltese:* real reports [1][2]. *Size:* big, and it needs someone to keep it up. *Needs:* calendar; an internet connection; the owner's OK. **Our view:** not worth it at first. Real dates (idea 5) already feel real, offline.

### C. Events
7. **"Hemm il-brim?": the onshore-wind swarm.** *You see:* the morning wind is Xlokk. By the afternoon the purple flag is up at Pretty Bay and Marsaskala, and the next morning the bay is full of mauve stingers. Bays on the other side are clear. *How:* each day has a wind. In swarm season, a swarm forms out at sea and drifts with the wind for about a day (in game) into the bays that face it. The warning comes first: the purple flag rises (idea 17) and a short chat line in Maltese and English ("Hemm il-brim f'Marsaskala!" †, "Jellyfish at Marsaskala!"). The swarm stays a day or two, then thins out or beaches (idea 9). *Why Maltese:* wind decides the bay [26][1][3]; the daily "safe bays" lists [27]. *Size:* big (wind, swarm movement, a list of bays with the way each faces). *Needs:* calendar; wind.
8. **The night glow.** *You see:* at night in a swarm, the sea surface is dotted with mauve bells, and each one flashes green when you swim into it or a wave hits it. Pretty, and a warning. *How:* mauve stingers sit near the surface at night and lower by day. A short green glow (particles, not a light block) when touched or in rough water. *Why Maltese:* [24]. *Size:* small. *Needs:* nothing.
9. **Beachings.** *You see:* after a swarm, the tideline is lined with dead jellyfish. Step on one barefoot and it still stings. *How:* a "beached jellyfish" block (flat, on sand) placed along the shore of the bay after a swarm. It stings like the live one but weaker, and rots away in a few game days. You can pick it up with a shovel. *Why Maltese:* [3][8][14]. *Size:* medium. *Needs:* nothing if beachings follow any swarm; wind for which shore.
10. **Man o' war alert.** *You see:* a rare chat line: "Portuguese man o' war seen off the south coast: get out of the water." A blue float drifts on the surface, its long tentacles trailing far below. *How:* a rare single mob, only March to May. It floats and drifts with the wind. Long tentacles hurt from up to several blocks below it. It strands on beaches and keeps stinging. *Why Maltese:* [6][7][9]. *Size:* medium. *Needs:* calendar for the season; wind helps.
11. **The new arrival.** *You see:* a big, pale jellyfish you have never met, alone. An advancement for the first one ("Alien in the water"). *How:* a very rare nomad jellyfish. Painful sting. It never swarms (no blooms yet in Malta [15]). Later the warming idea (idea 19) could let it bloom. *Why Maltese:* [10][15]. *Size:* small after the first species are built. *Needs:* nothing.

### D. Other ways to make them a hazard
12. **Stings by species.** *You feel:* each kind hurts differently. *How (vanilla effects):*
    - mauve stinger: 1 heart and Poison for about 4 seconds, plus a short "sting" mark (idea 13);
    - fried-egg: nothing, or a tiny itch with no damage;
    - barrel: Poison for 2 seconds at most;
    - crystal jellyfish: nothing;
    - by-the-wind sailor: no damage, a moment of itch;
    - man o' war: 2 hearts, Poison for 8 seconds, Slowness and Nausea for a few seconds;
    - nomad: 1.5 hearts and Poison for 6 seconds.

    Creative, spectator and peaceful are never stung, like the boll in [[Fish]]. The real stinger can scar for weeks [8], so the game sting should be short but annoying, never deadly. *Why Maltese:* sting strengths above. *Size:* small. *Needs:* nothing.
13. **The sting that lasts.** *You feel:* after a mauve stinger sting you have a red "Sting" effect for a while: it does no damage, but now and then the screen flashes and you lose a little hunger. Treated properly (idea 14) it goes at once. *How:* one new effect, Sting (*Ħruq* †). *Why Maltese:* the real sting swells for days [8]. *Size:* small. *Needs:* nothing.
14. **First aid the Maltese way.** *You do:* get out, splash sea water on it (use a water bottle filled from the sea: a "sea water bottle"), put on baking soda paste, scrape with a card, then ice. *How:* a **first aid kit** item (*kaxxa tal-ewwel għajnuna* †) crafted from a sea water bottle, baking soda and a card, which clears Sting in one use. Plain fresh water (a vanilla water bottle) **makes it worse**: the Sting lasts longer. For the man o' war the cure is a hot item instead (a hot water bottle, warmed at a furnace or the kenur). Vanilla milk still clears everything, as in vanilla. *Why Maltese:* [19][25][4][6][29]. *Size:* medium (new items and recipes). *Needs:* nothing. Vinegar is left out because Malta's current advice says no (question 5).
15. **Anti-jellyfish nets.** *You do:* craft net sections and set them out in shallow water to fence off a swimming area. Inside, no jellyfish. *How:* a net block that jellyfish can't pass, placed from the sea floor up to the surface, in water up to 3 blocks deep (the real nets went to 2.5 m [32]). Bought or crafted from string and floats. Over weeks it grows weed and needs a clean (optional: real nets were studied for fouling [32]). *Why Maltese:* the Pretty Bay net [11][32]. *Size:* medium. *Needs:* nothing.
16. **The lifeguard tower and the purple flag.** *You see:* a lifeguard tower on the big sandy beaches flying a flag: green, yellow, red, and **purple when jellyfish are near**. *How:* a flag block the player can place, or that comes with a lifeguard NPC later. It shows the bay's state: purple during a swarm (idea 7), red in a storm. Right-click it to hear "Hemm il-brim" or "Il-baħar nadif" (the sea is clear) †. *Why Maltese:* MTA flag colours [31]; the Golden Bay purple flag [19]. *Size:* small for the flag; medium with the NPC. *Needs:* wind or a swarm system to have anything to show.
17. **Spot the Jellyfish log.** *You do:* see a new kind, note it in a nature log (the same log idea 47 talks about for shearwaters). Each kind logged gives a small reward, and logging all seven gives an advancement. *How:* look at a jellyfish with a log item in hand. *Why Maltese:* the campaign's real way of reporting [1][2]. *Size:* small, or medium with the log item. *Needs:* nothing. Note: it must not use the University of Malta's name or logo in the game without permission, so it would be "a jellyfish log", not the real campaign.
18. **Turtles eat jellyfish.** *You see:* a sea turtle swims up and eats a jellyfish. Turtles near a beach mean fewer jellyfish. Throw a balloon or plastic in the sea and a turtle may eat it and get sick. *How:* either vanilla's turtle hunts our jellyfish (that changes vanilla, so behind a switch), or a Maltese loggerhead mob later (no switch needed). *Why Maltese:* turtles eat jellyfish, and mistake plastic for them [33]; the balloon death [34]. *Size:* small with vanilla turtles; big with a new loggerhead. *Needs:* nothing.
19. **Overfishing and warm water make it worse.** *You see:* if you and other players catch masses of fish in one stretch of coast, next season the swarms there are bigger. *How:* the mod counts fish caught per area over a season. Lots of fishing raises the bloom chance there for the next year. A run of hot summers could also let the nomad jellyfish start to bloom. *Why Maltese:* overfishing and warming are the causes Malta's scientists name [2][10]. *Size:* big. *Needs:* calendar. It also needs care so it doesn't feel like a punishment, since the owner prefers bonuses to penalties in other systems.

### Ways back (AGENTS.md rule 3)
All jellyfish are new mobs, so they don't change vanilla on their own. These still get a switch in `config/mimcontent.json`:
- **`jellyfish_in_vanilla_oceans`** (like the fish's `fish_in_vanilla_oceans`): jellyfish also in vanilla's oceans.
- **`jellyfish_stings`**: off makes every jellyfish harmless (for players who just want to look).
- **`jellyfish_swarms`**: off stops swarms, beachings and the flag changing (ideas 7, 9, 16).
- **`turtles_eat_jellyfish`**: needed for idea 18 with vanilla turtles, because it changes a vanilla mob.
- **`fresh_water_worsens_stings`**: idea 14's vanilla water bottle making a sting worse is a change to a vanilla item's use, so it gets a switch.
- **The live feed** (idea 6), if ever built: off by default.
Milk is left as in vanilla.

## Recommended first build
A small set that already feels Maltese and needs **no calendar and no wind yet**:
- **Three species:**
  - **mauve stinger** (the main hazard, with the night glow, idea 8);
  - **fried-egg jellyfish** (big, harmless, shows not every jellyfish is bad);
  - **Portuguese man o' war** (rare, floating, strong sting).
- **Stings by species** (idea 12) and the **Sting effect** (idea 13).
- **Beached jellyfish that still sting** (idea 9), left after a swarm.
- **Sea water first aid** (idea 14): the sea water bottle and the first aid kit.
- **A simple swarm** without wind: now and then a mauve stinger swarm gathers in one random bay, with a chat warning first. When the wind and the calendar arrive, the same swarm starts to follow them (ideas 1, 7).
- **The purple flag** block (idea 16), which turns purple when a swarm is in its bay.

Next, once the calendar exists: seasons and bloom years (ideas 1 to 3), the by-the-wind sailor beachings (idea 4) and nets (idea 15).

## Questions for the owner
1. **Which kinds first?** The three above (mauve stinger, fried-egg, man o' war), or also the by-the-wind sailor or the nomad jellyfish?
2. **How much should it hurt?** A short, annoying sting that never kills (suggested), or real danger from the man o' war?
3. **Before the calendar:** start with random swarms now, or wait until the calendar is built so jellyfish follow the seasons from day one?
4. **Wind:** build a simple wind of the day for the jellyfish now (it would later serve idea 51, the named winds), or keep swarms random until then?
5. **The cure:** use Malta's current advice (sea water, baking soda, card, ice; fresh water makes it worse; no vinegar)? If yes, idea 65's line "vinegar or a fresh-water rinse cures it" should be corrected.
6. **Turtles:** let vanilla turtles eat jellyfish (behind a switch), or wait for a Maltese loggerhead?
7. **Live data:** offline only (real date from the computer clock), or might an online "today's jellyfish" file on maltaminecraft.com be wanted one day?
8. **Nets and flags:** should players craft their own nets and flags, or should flags only come with lifeguards at the big beaches?
