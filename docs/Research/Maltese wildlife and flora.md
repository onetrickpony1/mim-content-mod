# Malta's living world, listed for the mod

**The Maltese Islands give a content mod a small but very distinctive cast: a few wild mammals, one endemic lizard with island-by-island races, four snakes, one frog, one freshwater crab found nowhere else, a famous set of night-time seabirds on Filfla and the cliffs, about 25 plants that grow only here, and a sea full of fish with their own Maltese names.** On land the wild mammals are few (hedgehog, rabbit, weasel, two shrews, four rodents and six to nine bats), but the Ice Age record from Għar Dalam adds dwarf elephants, dwarf hippos, giant dormice and a giant swan. The strongest "only in Malta" hooks are the merill (blue rock thrush, the national bird), the Filfla storm-petrel (the one bird named after Malta), the Maltese wall lizard, the qabru (national invertebrate), the widnet il-baħar (national plant), the għargħar (national tree), the Maltese swallowtail, the Bidni olive and the "Maltese fungus" of Fungus Rock. Food culture gives the mod its crops and dishes: lampuki caught under palm-frond rafts, octopus stew, capers, prickly pears, carobs, kunserva tomato paste, sulla fodder, and the indigenous Ġellewża and Girgentina grapes. Most of this is backed by University of Malta papers, BirdLife Malta, Nature Trust Malta, the official EU list of Maltese fish names and similar sources; a minority of entries, mainly plant names and some Maltese words, come from the researchers' general knowledge and are flagged so they can be checked before anything is built. The list below carries over every species from the five research notes, grouped by section, with a game note where the notes gave one.

## How to read the tables

Each table gives the English name, the Maltese name, the scientific name, the status (native, endemic, introduced, rare, extinct and so on), where it lives in Malta, and notes useful for game design. Maltese spelling (ħ, għ, ż, ċ) is copied exactly from the notes. Two marks show what still needs checking:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[BK]** | The whole entry comes from the researchers' background knowledge. Nothing about it was confirmed against a source. Check before use. |
| **[?]** | The entry is sourced, but this one detail (usually the Maltese name, a spelling, a status or a place) is unconfirmed, comes only from a search snippet or a weak source, or the sources disagree. |
| — | The notes give nothing for this field. |

Sources are linked under each table. "EU list" means the [EU commercial designations for Malta](https://fish-commercial-names.ec.europa.eu/fish-names/commercial-designations?lang=en&ms=MT&msLang=mt&page=1&rows=500&s=1&v=1), the most reliable source of Maltese fish and seafood names. "Checklist" means the official [Malta Bird Checklist 2026](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MALTA-BIRD-CHECKLIST-full-03.02.2026.pdf) (408 species), whose Maltese bird names win over village variants.

## Mammals: a handful of wild species, six to nine bats, and an Ice Age zoo

Malta has very few wild land mammals. Bats make up about half of the native land mammals ([FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/blog/bats/)). There is **no reliable record of a modern wild fox**: foxes, wolves and bears are known only as Pleistocene fossils from Għar Dalam ([Wikipedia: Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)). For scale, the islands measure Malta 245.7 km², Gozo 67.1 km², Comino 2.8 km², St Paul's Islands 10.1 ha, Cominotto 9.9 ha, Filfla 2.0 ha and Fungus Rock 0.7 ha ([Aloise et al. 2011](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)).

### Marine mammals

The Environment and Resources Authority (ERA) lists **four whales and four dolphins** in Maltese waters. Common, striped and bottlenose dolphins are "the most frequently recorded all year round"; the others prefer deep water. The **monk seal**, long counted as gone, has been **sighted again since 2024** (two sightings in May 2026, one in a sea cave). The Gozo place name Ta' Bumbarin and the sea cave Għar il-Bumarini probably remember it.

### Fossil and extinct animals (Għar Dalam and others)

Għar Dalam (Birżebbuġa, a Heritage Malta museum) is a cave about 144 m long, the first 50 m open to visitors since 1933. Its floor is layered like a history book: a hippo bone layer at the bottom, a "carnivore" layer with fox, wolf and bear, a deer layer, and on top the farm animals that arrived with people. Thieves took four dwarf-elephant tusks and a Neolithic child's skull in 1980. Names and dates are debated: the elephant is given as *P. falconeri* or *P. mnaidriensis* (molar studies suggest *falconeri* may not be at Għar Dalam), the lowest layers are dated from ~170,000 to "500,000+" years, and the dwarf deer died out ~18,000 or as late as ~4,000 years ago.

## Birds: 18 regular breeders, a seabird stronghold, and 230 species a year passing through

BirdLife Malta says **about 18 wild species breed regularly** (11 all year), with 27 more nesting now and then. The official checklist holds **408 species**, but only about **230 turn up in an average year** (235 in 2022). Malta is "the only country in Europe and the Mediterranean with no regularly breeding birds of prey", because of illegal shooting ([BirdLife Malta, hunting](https://birdlifemalta.org/information/hunting/)). The big numbers are seabirds that come to the cliffs only at night. All Maltese bird names below are from the official checklist.

### Migrants and winter visitors

Spring passage peaks **March to mid-May** (best on Comino and over the Comino Channel, watched from Ċirkewwa). Autumn passage runs **mid-August to November**, with raptor passage peaking about **20 September** at the **Buskett** roost. In winter the countryside holds mostly robins, chiffchaffs, blackcaps, stonechats, black redstarts, white wagtails, meadow pipits and starlings; Buskett is "practically the only safe haven" for wintering thrushes and finches (MBR22).

### Finches, quarry birds and the hunting-and-trapping culture

About **7,969 hunters** were licensed for spring 2025. Spring hunting covers quail (Summiena) and turtle dove (Gamiema); autumn 2026 allows 40 land species and 12 at sea. **Clap nets** (two large nets on the ground that swing shut) with live decoys are used for song thrush and golden plover, the only legally trappable species in 2026. Finch trapping under a "research" derogation was ruled illegal by the EU Court in 2018 and 2024. Keeping and breeding finches in cages is a widespread pastime, so many wild sightings are escapes. A neutral game design (bird ringing and birdwatching stations at Għadira, Simar, Buskett and Comino) fits the real conservation story without endorsing illegal activity.

## Reptiles and amphibians: one endemic lizard in island races, four snakes, one frog

Malta's wild land reptiles are the endemic **Maltese wall lizard** (with separate races on Filfla, Fungus Rock and Selmunett), two geckos, the ocellated skink, the Mediterranean chameleon and four snakes. **All four snakes are harmless to people and protected by law** ([BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)). There is a single amphibian, the painted frog.

### Lampuki season, fishing and fish dishes

The lampuki season opens about **15 August** (Santa Marija): about 130 licensed boats leave together at midnight, mostly from **Marsaxlokk** and **Mġarr (Gozo)**, and fishing runs to the end of December ([Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/it-s-lampuki-season-take-a-look-at-this-local-tradition-associated-with-malta-s-small-scale-fishing-sector)). Fishers set **kannizzati**: floating rafts with **palm fronds** that give shade, anchored by a heavy **limestone block**; once fish gather under them the nets go in. Each licensee lays at least 35 rafts ([Wikipedia: Lampuki netting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampuki_netting); [FAO](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/DOCUMENT/fcp/en/FI_CP_MT.pdf)). Boats are blessed at Marsaxlokk at the start of the season, catches are reported down by half, and line fishing is rising. Signature dishes: **torta tal-lampuki** (lampuki pie with spinach, cauliflower, olives, capers and tomato, Sept–Dec), **aljotta** (garlic-and-tomato fish soup with rice and herbs, a Lent dish), **stuffat tal-qarnit** (octopus stew with Maltese bread, also in spaghetti) and **klamari mimlija** (stuffed squid) ([Nomad Paradise](https://nomadparadise.com/maltese-food); [Kayak food guide](https://www.kayak.co.uk/magazine/malta-food-guide); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/what-to-eat-and-drink-in-malta)). The luzzu boat's colours and eye on the bow, sea-urchin roe pasta (ricci) and qarnit bit-tewm are well known but were not sourced [?].

## Land bugs, spiders and snails: 4,500 recorded species and a dozen endemic snails

Malta has about **4,500 recorded land and freshwater arthropods** ([Cassar 2020](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/fragmenta_entomologica/article/download/462/436)) and an estimated **6,000–8,000 insects** in all. By group: beetles more than 1,700; Lepidoptera about two dozen butterflies and about 600 moths; bees and wasps possibly over 2,000; flies 600 identified (perhaps 1,000); true bugs about 600 ([Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)). Beetle families: 182 weevils, 23 bark beetles, 54 scarabs, 14 Pselaphinae. Spiders: 137 (2005, 7 endemic), 141 (2018) or 150 species in 31 families. Non-marine molluscs: 62 land and 15 freshwater snails plus 2 pea clams. Dragonflies: 18. A secondary source gives 55 endemic animals, 81 % of them arthropods, though some "endemics" have since been found elsewhere. Butterflies, dragonflies, mantises, the scorpion and the endemic snails are small enough groups to model in full; beetles, moths, flies and spiders need a short list.

## Plants: about 25 endemics, two national emblems and a farming landscape

Malta has roughly **1,100 native, ancient and naturalised plant species** (a 2013 UM figure seen only in a search summary [?]). ### Trees, woodland and large shrubs

Native woodland was "almost totally exterminated"; the remnants are "living fossils of ancient forests" (NTM). **Buskett** is semi-natural woodland, largely planted, where trees regenerate naturally. **Ballut tal-Wardija** is the largest remnant, with holm oaks thought to be **500–900 years old**. Afforestation woods (none natural): Miżieb, L-Aħrax tal-Mellieħa, Pietà, Fort Chambray (Mġarr, Gozo), Kennedy Grove (Salini), Wied Għollieqa.

### Fungi, the "Maltese fungus", lichens and mosses

The famous **"Maltese fungus" is not a fungus**: it is *Cynomorium coccineum*, a dark-red parasitic flowering plant with a repulsive smell that grows on top of **Fungus Rock** (Il-Ġebla tal-Ġeneral, "the General's Rock", Dwejra, Gozo; a 60 m limestone stack, now a nature reserve). The Knights called it *fungus melitensis*, used it to stop bleeding and cure dysentery, and sent it to European royalty. In **1746 Grand Master Pinto** banned anyone from the rock on pain of three years rowing in the galleys, posted a permanent guard, ran a cable-car basket 50 m to the mainland and had the sides smoothed to remove handholds; Dwejra Tower (1651) was built partly to guard it (date and punishment rest on one or two sources [?]). The rock also hosts the national plant.

Conservation-sensitive species (seahorses, qabru, monk seal, date mussel, pen shell, storm-petrels, protected snakes and lizards, the national plant and tree) suit a "protect, don't harvest" design: observable, perhaps with a penalty or a ringing/recording reward, rather than food or loot. Hunting and trapping are politically sensitive in Malta, so a bird-ringing or birdwatching mechanic fits the real story better than a trapping one.
