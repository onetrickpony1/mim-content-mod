# Land vertebrates of the Maltese Islands (mammals, reptiles, amphibians, freshwater fish; domestic breeds; fossil fauna)

Researched 2026-11-14 for the MiM content mod. Scope: Malta, Gozo, Comino, Cominotto, Filfla, Fungus Rock, St Paul's Islands (Selmunett). Marine mammals, sea turtles and birds are out of scope (noted only where a source mixes them in).

Short reading guide for the report writer: Malta has few wild land vertebrates. Roughly: ~6 to 9 resident bats, 1 hedgehog, 2 shrews, 1 weasel, 1 rabbit, 4 rodents; 1 lizard (endemic, with islet subspecies), 2 geckos, 1 skink, 1 chameleon, 4 snakes; 1 frog; 1 native freshwater-ish fish (a brackish killifish). Rank of sources: peer-reviewed University of Malta / journal papers > BirdLife Malta / Nature Trust Malta > Wikipedia and popular sites.

## Which mammals live wild in Malta, and which are extinct?

### Takeaway
The wild land mammals are: Algerian hedgehog, Sicilian shrew (Gozo/Comino only), Etruscan shrew, least weasel, wild rabbit, black rat, brown rat, house mouse and wood mouse, plus bats. Bats are about half the native land mammals; the count of resident bat species is disputed (6, 7 or 9), and the greater horseshoe bat is probably extinct locally. I found no reliable source for a modern wild red fox in Malta; foxes are known only as Pleistocene fossils from Għar Dalam.

### Cited Findings

**Overall list (Maltese checklist as given in a 2011 peer-reviewed paper)**
- Terrestrial mammals of the Maltese Islands that are also on Comino: *Rattus norvegicus*, *R. rattus*, *Mus musculus*, *Apodemus sylvaticus*, *Oryctolagus cuniculus* and the bat *Pipistrellus kuhlii*. Those not yet recorded on Comino: *Atelerix algirus fallax*, *Crocidura sicula*, *Suncus etruscus*, the non-migratory bats *Rhinolophus hipposideros minimus*, *R. ferrumequinum*, *Myotis blythi punicus*, *Plecotus austriacus*, *Pipistrellus pygmaeus*; *Mustela nivalis* is also not recorded on Comino (citing Baldacchino & Schembri 2002) — [Aloise, Baldacchino & Amori 2011, Biodiversity Journal 2(3):145-148](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)
- Island areas from the same paper (useful for scale): Malta 245.7 km², Gozo 67.1 km², Comino 2.8 km², St Paul's Islands 10.1 ha, Cominotto 9.9 ha, Filfla 2.0 ha, Fungus Rock 0.7 ha; total 316 km² — [Aloise et al. 2011](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)
- Friends of the Earth Malta / National Museum of Natural History "Atlas of Maltese Mammals" lists: Algerian hedgehog *Atelerix algirus*, Etruscan shrew *Suncus etruscus*, Sicilian shrew *Crocidura sicula calypso*, wild rabbit *Oryctolagus cuniculus*, weasel *Mustela nivalis*, and "four rodents"; says bats are about 50% of Malta's indigenous terrestrial mammals, with 9 resident bat species plus 4 migrants/winter visitors (not named) — [FoE Malta, Atlas of Maltese Mammals – Bats](https://foemalta.org/blog/bats/)
- Wikipedia's "List of mammals of Malta" is poor for this purpose: it omits the rabbit and all rodents, includes cetaceans and the extirpated Mediterranean monk seal (*Monachus monachus*), and marks the weasel as "possibly introduced" — [Wikipedia, List of mammals of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Malta)

**Hedgehog**
- Algerian (North African) hedgehog *Atelerix algirus* (Maltese subspecies cited as *A. a. fallax*); Maltese name **Qanfud** (BirdLife). Lives in fallow fields; digs under rubble walls; forages for snails, slugs and insects among the stones; many are killed on country roads at night — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- Alternative Maltese name **xummiemu** (synonym of qanfud) — [Wiktionary mirror, qanfud](https://wiki.neonvagabond.xyz/content/wiktionary_en_all_nopic_2024-05/A/qanfud); a popular article notes the Maltese hedgehog is "not actually Maltese but Algerian" and that many Maltese say Xummiemu — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/introducing-the-cutest-protected-species-il-qanfud-the-maltese-hedgehog)
- IUCN: Least Concern — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Malta)

**Shrews**
- Sicilian shrew *Crocidura sicula*: endemic to the Siculo-Maltese archipelago. In the Maltese Islands recorded only from **Gozo** (subspecies *C. s. calypso* Hutterer 1991, not recognised by all authors), plus a 2005 owl-pellet record from **Comino** (first for Comino). Fossil bones show it once lived on mainland Malta, where it is now extinct for unknown reasons (Hutterer 2005). Etruscan shrew *Suncus etruscus* occurs on Malta. Older literature called the Gozo shrews *C. suaveolens* or *C. russula* — [Aloise et al. 2011](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)

**Weasel**
- Least weasel *Mustela nivalis*, Maltese **Ballottra**: "the only carnivorous land mammal in the Maltese Islands"; shy and secretive; eats rodents, rabbits and nestlings; scarce; protected by law; "little longer than a pen" — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- Status disputed: Wikipedia says "possibly introduced" — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Malta)

**Rabbit**
- Wild rabbit *Oryctolagus cuniculus*, Maltese **Fenek Selvaġġ** (fenek = rabbit). Prolific; hunted in summer but numbers stable; wary; in burrows by day, feeds at dawn and dusk; leaves droppings piles as territory markers and scrapes at the base of shrubs to dig roots; garrigue habitat; "many wild rabbits on Kemmuna" (Comino) — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- Rabbit and rat remains appear in the Holocene layer II of Għar Dalam (with domestic animals) — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)
- The Pharaoh Hound's Maltese name is **Kelb tal-Fenek** ("rabbit dog") — [Wikipedia, Pharaoh Hound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh_Hound)

**Rodents**
- Checklist: *Apodemus sylvaticus* (wood mouse), *Rattus rattus* (black rat), *R. norvegicus* (brown rat), *Mus musculus* (house mouse); wood mouse identity was settled late (unidentified in a 1981 owl-pellet study); house mouse now often split as *Mus domesticus*, so old records use either name — [search summary of Maltese checklists / 1980 Gozo barn owl pellet paper](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/39203/1/Mammalian_content_of_barn_owl_pellets_from_Gozo_1980.pdf); [Aloise et al. 2011](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)
- Rats are a problem on islets: on Selmunett most rats were *Rattus rattus*, one *R. norvegicus* seen; rats were eradicated from Selmunett in 2006-2007 — [Sciberras & Schembri 2008, Herpetological Bulletin 105](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/21170/1/Sciberras%20%26%20Schembri%20%282008%29%20Herpetol%20Bull%20105%20pp28-34%20with%20figs.pdf). A black-rat eradication was also studied for Comino (shearwater colonies) — [Pascal 2008, Comino black rat eradication](https://initiative-pim.org/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Pascal-M._2008_Comino_Site-of-Comino-Malta-Eradication-of-the-Black-Rat-Why-Is-it-possible-Is-it-urgent.pdf)

**Extinct / extirpated mammals**
- Mediterranean monk seal *Monachus monachus*: extirpated (marine, but used to haul out in sea caves) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Malta)
- Red fox *Vulpes vulpes*, wolf *Canis lupus*, brown bear *Ursus* cf. *arctos*: only in Pleistocene layer V of Għar Dalam — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)

### Inferences
- *Pipistrellus pygmaeus* in older lists and *P. pipistrellus* in the 2019 genetic list are probably the same Maltese population re-identified; a mod should pick one "pipistrelle" mob. 
- A game-ready mammal set: hedgehog, rabbit, weasel, shrew, wood mouse, rat, 2-3 bat types (horseshoe, pipistrelle, long-eared). The red fox should be a fossil/"Ice Age" mob only, not a modern spawn.
- Hedgehog, rabbit and weasel are nocturnal/crepuscular and tied to rubble walls (ħitan tas-sejjieħ), fallow fields and garrigue; bats to caves (e.g. Għar Dalam), Buskett woodland and old buildings.

## Which reptiles live in Malta, and which are endemic subspecies?

### Takeaway
Wild land reptiles: the endemic Maltese wall lizard (*Podarcis filfolensis*, four named Maltese races, including Filfla's and Fungus Rock's), two geckos, the ocellated skink, the Mediterranean chameleon, and four snakes (western whip snake and leopard snake native; Algerian whip snake and cat snake naturalised/introduced, cat snake status disputed but a 2026 genetic study says introduced from Greece). Released pond terrapins (sliders, map turtles) are introduced pets.

### Cited Findings

**Maltese wall lizard, *Podarcis filfolensis* (Bedriaga 1876)** — Maltese **Gremxula**
- The only living lacertid in the Maltese Islands; endemic to the Maltese Islands plus Linosa and Lampione (Pelagian Islands). Races: *filfolensis* on **Filfla**; *maltensis* (Mertens 1921) on Malta, Gozo and Comino; *generalensis* (Gulia in Despott 1915) on **Fungus Rock (General's Rock)**; *kieselbachi* (Fejérváry 1924) on **Selmunett (St Paul's Islands)**; *laurentiimuelleri* on Linosa and Lampione. A Cominotto population has also been treated as distinct. Genetic differences between races are small; races differ in body size, dark markings and males' throat colour; closest relative is *Podarcis sicula* — [Sciberras & Schembri 2008](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/21170/1/Sciberras%20%26%20Schembri%20%282008%29%20Herpetol%20Bull%20105%20pp28-34%20with%20figs.pdf)
- Selmunett race: counts fell from 18 lizards/hour (1999) to zero (2005), caused by rat predation; rats eradicated 2006-2007; captive lizards (taken 2004) released June 2007; outcome uncertain. Selmunett is a protected site and the lizard a protected species — [Sciberras & Schembri 2008](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/21170/1/Sciberras%20%26%20Schembri%20%282008%29%20Herpetol%20Bull%20105%20pp28-34%20with%20figs.pdf). Other secondary sources say the Selmunett race "became extinct in 2005" — [search summary, cavac mirror](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Filfola%20lizard) (contradicted/softened by the 2008 paper)
- A MaltaToday piece argues the subspecies names are not valid (morphology only) — [MaltaToday, Off to see the lizards](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/2962/off-to-see-the-lizards)
- Look and behaviour: females brown; males with **yellow throat and emerald-green back speckled black**; suns early morning on stones and walls; males fight over territories in spring; eats ants and small insects; drops its tail to escape; commoner in gardens than countryside lately; many killed by cats — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- IUCN Least Concern (Corti et al. 2008) — [Wikipedia, List of reptiles of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_Malta)

**Geckos** — Maltese **Wiżgħa**
- Two species: Mediterranean (Turkish) house gecko *Hemidactylus turcicus* and Moorish gecko *Tarentola mauritanica* — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_Malta); [RepFocus Malta](https://repfocus.dk/GEO/Malta.html)
- Nocturnal; climb walls and ceilings with toe pads; eat flies, moths, silverfish (kamla), spiders, mosquitoes; on summer evenings make "creaky mating calls, which sound like tiny barks"; subject of folk myths and phobias — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)

**Ocellated skink, *Chalcides ocellatus*** — Maltese **Xaħmet l-Art**
- Only skink in Malta; tiny legs, wriggles like a snake; glossy brown skin mottled with black-and-white eye-spots; eats ants and bugs; hides in rubble-wall holes; protected — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- Also found in Għar Dalam's Holocene layer II — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)

**Mediterranean chameleon, *Chamaeleo chamaeleon*** — listed (IUCN LC) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_Malta); snakes prey on chameleons — [BirdLife Malta](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)

**Snakes** (four species; all harmless to humans and protected by law — [BirdLife Malta](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf))
| Species | Maltese name | Status | Notes |

**Freshwater turtles / tortoises**
- Pond slider / red-eared slider *Trachemys scripta (elegans)* and false map turtle *Graptemys pseudogeographica* listed (released pets, introduced) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_Malta)
- European pond turtle *Emys orbicularis*: Pleistocene layers IV-V of Għar Dalam only; a tortoise *Testudo* sp. in the Holocene layer II — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)
- Sea turtles (loggerhead *Caretta caretta* etc.) occur at sea; loggerheads nest occasionally on Maltese beaches (out of scope; Wikipedia list includes them, and mislabels hawksbill as *Dermochelys*) — [Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_Malta); [RepFocus](https://repfocus.dk/GEO/Malta.html)

### Inferences
- Wall-lizard variants are a natural fit for a game: one base lizard with island variants — green/black-spotted Malta form, a larger dark/melanic-looking Filfla form (Filfla race is famous for being big and dark; size/colour detail not verified this session), Fungus Rock and Selmunett forms as rare "secret" variants.
- Four snakes give clear visuals: black whip snake (common, fast, daytime), spotted leopard snake, greenish Algerian whip snake (harbour towns only), nocturnal silver-and-black cat snake.

### Gaps
- Chameleon status: widely said to be introduced (c. 19th century) and concentrated in the north-west (e.g. Għadira/Mellieħa, Buskett?) — not verified this session; Maltese name (probably **kamaleonte**) not verified.
- Filfla lizard size and colour, and whether a Cominotto race name exists, not verified.
- Maltese names of the two geckos separately (wiżgħa is generic) not found.

## Amphibians, freshwater fish and the freshwater crab

### Takeaway
One amphibian: the painted frog (Żrinġ), in the few valleys with water. One native fish of inland/brackish water: the Mediterranean killifish (Bużaqq), now in very few sites (Salina natural; Għadira and Simar introduced). The Maltese freshwater crab (Qabru) lives only in a few permanent streams.

### Cited Findings
- **Painted frog** *Discoglossus pictus*, Maltese **Żrinġ**: freshwater habitat is one of Malta's rarest; adults survive summer buried in mud, need rain, streams and pools to breed; catching tadpoles is illegal — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf). Also in Għar Dalam layer II with green toad *Bufo viridis*; common toad *Bufo bufo* in Pleistocene layers IV-V — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)
- Valleys with water named in the same factfile (white poplar sites): **Wied il-Luq (Buskett), Wied il-Fiddien (near Rabat), Wied Qlejgħa (Chadwick Lakes)** — [BirdLife Malta](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- **Freshwater crab** *Potamon fluviatile lanfrancoi* (scientific name from general knowledge, see Gaps), Maltese **Qabru**: rarer than the frog; only where water runs all year, e.g. **Wied il-Baħrija** and **Wied il-Lunzjata (Gozo)**; eats snails and tadpoles; Maltese form "slightly different from the ones in other regions"; protected — [BirdLife Malta Wildlife Factfile](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WILDLIFE-FACTFILE.pdf)
- **Mediterranean killifish** *Aphanius fasciatus* (Maltese **Bużaqq**): 2002 survey found it at nine localities; large populations at **Salina**, the **Simar** and **Għadira** reserves and reservoirs at **Marsa** and Għadira; Simar and Għadira populations introduced from Salina and Marsa stock; Salina (and maybe Marsa) the only natural populations left — [Present distribution of the threatened killifish (UM)](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/5346/1/Present%20distribution%20of%20the%20threatened%20killifish.pdf) (via search summary)
- Endangered in Malta; MAFA + Nature Trust Malta + ERA captive-breeding project; 80 fish taken under emergency permit as a backup stock against heatwave die-offs at Salina — [Aquatic Resources Malta](https://aquaticresources.gov.mt/?p=615)
- Distribution "regressed sharply" despite legal protection (MEDCOAST 2011) — [UM paper](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/26177/1/C1-B41-S435-S444.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Freshwater content for a mod is tiny and site-specific: frogs + crabs in a handful of winter-wet valleys (Chadwick Lakes, Wied il-Baħrija, Wied il-Luq at Buskett, Wied il-Lunzjata on Gozo), killifish in salt-marsh pools (Salina, Għadira, Simar). This matches the project's valley/reed-bed notes.

### Gaps
- Painted frog subspecies (*D. p. pictus*) and abundance not confirmed.
- Introduced fish (mosquitofish *Gambusia*, goldfish/carp in reservoirs, eels *Anguilla anguilla* in Chadwick Lakes) — no sources found; treat as unverified.
- Freshwater crab scientific name not confirmed this session.

## Maltese domestic breeds

### Takeaway
Recognised Maltese heritage breeds: Pharaoh Hound (Kelb tal-Fenek), Maltese goat, Maltese sheep, Maltese ox (cattle), Maltese Black chicken; all livestock breeds are rare and under a 2025 government/NGO (Breeds of Origin Conservancy) conservation programme. I found no source on a Maltese donkey or pig breed this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Pharaoh Hound**, Maltese **Kelb tal-Fenek** (rabbit dog) — [Wikipedia, Pharaoh Hound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh_Hound)
- **Maltese goat**: traditional dairy goat; fewer than 1,000 native goats left (Breeds of Origin Conservancy estimate); origins debated; national initiative launched 31 Oct 2025 with BOC — [Wikipedia, Maltese goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_goat); [IGA overview paper](https://www.iga-goatworld.com/uploads/6/1/6/2/6162024/an_overview_of_the_indigenous_goat_of_the_maltese_islands_through_various_literature.pdf)
- **Maltese sheep**: white body, black head (like the Maltese goat); local breeds declined strongly; reintroduction programme from 1994 — [CIHEAM 1995](https://ressources.ciheam.org/ressources/om/pdf/c11/96605546.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Maltese ox / cattle**: 2015 mtDNA study; Neolithic bull images show long horns and a hump (zebu-like); no proof of link to modern breed — [Lancioni et al. ASPA 2015](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22991/1/Lancioni_ASPA2015_Maltesecattle-1.pdf). Maltese Ox and Maltese Black Chicken are the animals eligible for Maltese-origin genetic-resource certificates — [servizz.gov.mt](https://www.servizz.gov.mt/en/Pages/Environment_-Energy_-Agriculture-and-Fisheries/Agriculture/Horticulture/WEB05291/default.aspx)
- **Maltese Black chicken**: rustic native black hen replaced by commercial breeds by 1970; at risk of extinction; breed standard published 2014 (World's Poultry Science Journal); BOC in-situ breeding from 2024 at Tulliera farm (Delimara), Ta' Gużman (Siġġiewi) and the government experimental farm in Gozo — [MaltaToday](https://maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/128751/farmers_hope_to_reverse_extinction_risk_of_maltese_black_chicken); [UM paper](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar//bitstream/123456789/22994/1/S0043933914000397a.pdf)
- BOC got a 15-year lease of a public site at **Delimara** as a centre for indigenous breeds — [Maltadaily](https://maltadaily.mt/malta-embarks-on-major-initiative-to-protect-and-revive-its-living-cultural-heritage)
- Holocene Għar Dalam layer II holds cow, sheep, goat, pig, cat and equine remains (the early farm animals) — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)

### Inferences
- The Pharaoh Hound + wild rabbit pairing is the strongest cultural hook (rabbit hunting, stuffat tal-fenek). The goat, sheep (white with black head) and black chicken are easy vanilla-style variants.

## Fossil and prehistoric fauna (Għar Dalam and other sites)

### Takeaway
Għar Dalam (Birżebbuġa; Heritage Malta museum) holds a layered record: Middle Pleistocene hippo bone breccia with dwarf elephants, giant dormice and a giant swan; a "Carnivora" layer with fox, wolf and bear; a late-Pleistocene layer of dwarf deer, voles and pond turtles; then Holocene farm animals. Names and dates are debated.

### Cited Findings
- Layers (Wikipedia, citing the stratigraphy literature):
  - **VII Bone breccia / Hippopotamus layer** (Middle Pleistocene, before 165,900 years): hippos *Hippopotamus pentlandi* and *H. melitensis*, dwarf elephant (*Palaeoloxodon mnaidriensis* and/or *Palaeoloxodon* sp.), deer *Cervus* cf. *elaphus*, shrew, dormice (*Maltamys*/*Eliomys*), bats, birds incl. giant swan *Cygnus falconeri*.
  - **V Lower Red Earth / Carnivora stage** (167,300-151,200 years): dwarf elephant, *H. pentlandi*, deer, **red fox *Vulpes vulpes*, wolf *Canis lupus*, brown bear *Ursus* cf. *arctos***, vole *Pitymys melitensis*, shrew, bats, pond turtle *Emys orbicularis*, common toad, birds.
  - **IV Upper Red Earth / Cervus stage** (Late Pleistocene, after ~116,000-104,000 years to ~20,000): probably 3 endemic dwarf deer *Cervus* species, *Bos* sp., *Equus* sp., vole, shrew, bats, *Emys orbicularis*, *Bufo bufo*, birds.
  - **II Holocene** (2,700 years to recent): cow, sheep, goat, pig, cat, horse/ass, black rat, house mouse, rabbit, shrew, wood mouse, tortoise, ocellated skink, painted frog, green toad; human remains, pottery.
  — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)
- Cave ~144 m long, first 50 m open; investigated by Issel 1865, Cooke 1892; open to public March 1933; museum set up by curator Joseph Baldacchino; WWII air-raid shelter; 1980 theft of four dwarf elephant tusks and a Neolithic child's skull; until 2025 thought the earliest evidence of humans (~7,400 years) — [Wikipedia, Għar Dalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C4%A7ar_Dalam)
- Conflicts: elephant called *P. falconeri* or *P. mnaidriensis*, and molar analysis suggests *P. falconeri* may not be present at Għar Dalam; giant dormouse given as *Leithia cartei* or *L. melitensis*; dates for the lowest layers range from ~170,000 to "500,000+" years; deer extinction given as ~18,000 years or as late as ~4,000 years (Chalcolithic) — [search summary of Wikipedia, showcaves.com, Xjenza 1998 sediment review](https://www.xjenza.org/JOURNAL-XJENZA/1998-3-1/01-Ghar-Dalam-Cave-A-Review-of-the-Sediments-on-the-Cave-Floor-Stratigraphy.pdf); [showcaves.com](https://www.showcaves.com/english/mt/showcaves/Dalam.html)
- Giant swan *Cygnus falconeri* co-occurs on Malta with *Leithia*, *Maltamys*, the giant tortoise *Solitudo* and *Discoglossus* (search summary) — [Palaeoloxodon falconeri mirror](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Palaeoloxodon%20falconeri) (low-quality mirror; verify)
- The Sicilian shrew's past presence on Malta mainland is shown by fossil bones — [Aloise et al. 2011](https://biodiversityjournal.com/images/pubblicazioni/biodiversity-journal-2011/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03/biodiversity-journal-2011-02-03_145-148.pdf)

### Inferences
- Strong "Ice Age Malta" mob set: dwarf elephant (about pony size), dwarf hippo, giant dormouse, giant swan, dwarf deer, giant tortoise, plus fox/wolf/bear; Għar Dalam as a fossil-dig structure.
