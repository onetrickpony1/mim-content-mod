# Birds of the Maltese Islands (Malta, Gozo, Comino, Filfla and the islets)

Research notes for a Minecraft content mod that adds Maltese animals. Written 2026-11-14.

**How to read these notes**
- Maltese names are copied exactly from the official **Malta Bird Checklist** of the Malta Rarities and Records Committee (BirdLife Malta, updated Jan 2026, 408 species): [Checklist 2026](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MALTA-BIRD-CHECKLIST-full-03.02.2026.pdf). Where an older or village name differs, the checklist name wins. A 1971 *Il-Merill* note warns that "previous authorities gave one or more Maltese names to the same species and often these names varied from one village to another" ([Il-Merill 5, 1971](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/40529/1/Il-Merill_05_1971.pdf), via search summary).
- Months and places come mostly from the BirdLife Malta annual **Malta Bird Report 2022** ([MBR 2022](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/MALTA-BIRD-REPORT-2022-digital.pdf)) and **Malta Bird Report 2023** ([MBR 2023](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MALTA-BIRD-REPORT-2023-2024-online.pdf)). These are one-year records, so months are "typical" not absolute.
- Size, colour and song notes marked **(general)** are standard field-guide knowledge, not from a Malta source. They are put in the Inferences sections, never presented as cited findings.
- Short source keys used in the tables: **C** = Checklist 2026; **MBR22**, **MBR23** = the bird reports; **SB** = [LIFE+ Malta Seabird Project report](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Malta-Seabird-Project-Laymans-Report-ENG.pdf); **R16** = [BirdLife reserve breeding list 2016](https://birdlifemalta.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/List-of-breeding-species-at-BirdLife-Maltas-Nature-Reserves.pdf); **BB19** = [BirdLife "A good year so far for Malta's breeding birds" 2019](https://birdlifemalta.org/2019/06/a-good-year-so-far-for-maltas-breeding-birds/); **S&G70** = [Sultana & Gauci, "The Breeding Birds of Malta", Il-Merill 1, 1970](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/40835/1/The_breeding_birds_of_Malta_1970.pdf).

## What is the full list of breeding birds in Malta and their numbers?

### Takeaway
Malta has a very short breeding list: BirdLife Malta says **about 18 wild species breed regularly** (11 year-round residents), with **27 more nesting irregularly or occasionally**. The big numbers are seabirds: European storm-petrel (5,000–8,000 pairs on Filfla, or 11,000–16,000 nationally by a newer estimate), Scopoli's shearwater (4,500–5,000 pairs) and Yelkouan shearwater (1,660–1,980 pairs, ~10 % of the world). On land the Spanish sparrow, Sardinian warbler and blue rock thrush are everywhere. Malta has almost no breeding birds of prey because of illegal shooting. I found no national per-species pair table for land birds (the 2008 and 2018 atlases hold them but I could not open them).

### Cited Findings

**Overall size of the list**
- "About 18 wild species of birds are regularly breeding in Malta", 11 of them sedentary residents (named: Blue Rock Thrush, Cetti's Warbler, Spanish Sparrow); summer breeders named: Tree Sparrow (also called resident on the same page), Spotted Flycatcher, Little Ringed Plover, Black-winged Stilt, Common Swift, Pallid Swift; "27 other species nest irregularly or occasionally" (not named), [BirdLife Malta breeding birds project](https://birdlifemalta.org/appreciating-protecting-and-understanding-local-breeding-bird-species-in-malta/)
- National atlases: Malta Breeding Bird Atlas 2008 (BirdLife Malta 2009) and Malta Breeding Bird Atlas 2018 (Epsilon Malta 2019, census 2017–2018, published by the Wild Birds Regulation Unit), [EBBA2 national atlases](https://ebba2.info/about/national-atlases/?country=MT). Book: Sultana, Borg, Gauci & Falzon (2011) *The Breeding Birds of Malta* (cited in Maltese reports; not opened).
- BirdLife Malta (Feb 2026) refers to "the recently published national Breeding Bird Atlas" (no title or year given), [BirdLife Malta, barn owl 2026](https://birdlifemalta.org/2026/02/another-barn-owl-from-reintroduction-project-shot-as-species-remains-locally-extinct/)
- In 1970, "Out of the 32 species that Despott listed [in 1916], only 14 still breed regularly", S&G70
- Malta is "the only country in Europe and the Mediterranean with no regularly breeding birds of prey", [BirdLife Malta hunting page](https://birdlifemalta.org/information/hunting/)
- Breeding-pair totals quoted from the atlases conflict: one report gives the 2008 total as 12,736–16,998 pairs, another 2,039–5,728 pairs, and the 2018 atlas 2,151–6,434 pairs (1,400–4,180 in Gozo). These figures came only through search-result snippets of an ERA Appropriate Assessment ([ERA AA 2024](https://era.org.mt/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/PA07333_22-AA-Report_v3.pdf), not opened, the site refuses downloads). They are probably for one species, not all birds. **Treat as unreliable.**
- Spanish sparrow: "Raine, Sultana and Gillings (2009) reported 100,251–295,826 pairs of P. hispaniolensis for Malta", same ERA document, search snippet only.

### Inferences
- A practical "breeding bird" set for the mod (regular, visible): merill, Spanish sparrow, tree sparrow, Sardinian warbler, Cetti's warbler, zitting cisticola, spectacled warbler, collared dove, moorhen, yellow-legged gull, storm-petrel, Scopoli's shearwater, Yelkouan shearwater, common and pallid swift, short-toed lark, spotted flycatcher, little ringed plover, barn swallow, kestrel, chukar (Comino). That is about 21, which fits BirdLife's "about 18 regular" plus the Comino chukar and a few recent colonists.
- Game notes **(general)**: merill male is slate blue (female brown, scaly), starling-sized (21–23 cm, sourced: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_rock_thrush)), sings fluting phrases from cliff tops and rooftops, nests in rock holes and walls. Spanish sparrow male has a chestnut crown and heavy black-streaked flanks; flocks and noisy roosts in town trees. Sardinian warbler: small grey warbler, male black cap and red eye-ring, harsh rattling alarm call. Cetti's warbler: brown, hidden in reeds, sudden explosive song. Zitting cisticola: tiny streaky brown bird, "zit-zit" song in bouncing song-flight over fields. Moorhen: black with red-and-yellow bill, white under-tail flicks, swims. Yellow-legged gull: large white gull, grey back, yellow legs and bill with red spot, loud laughing calls.
- Seabird behaviour for game design (from SB): shearwaters and storm-petrels come to land **only at night** to nest in cliff crevices, caves and boulder scree; they are hard to see by day but loud at night. Ideal for a "night-only cliff mob" that spawns on sea-cliff faces and flies over the sea.
- The breeding list is dominated by cliff and town species; woodland breeders are tied to the few groves (Buskett, Ta' Qali), and wetland breeders to three small reserves (Għadira, Simar, Salina) plus a few valleys (Wied Għajn Riħana / Chadwick Lakes).

### Gaps
- No per-species national pair estimates for land birds: the 2008 and 2018 atlases, Sultana et al. (2011) and Malta's Birds Directive Article 12 report (2013–2018; 2019–2024 due) were not available online in readable form. ERA files refused downloads (HTTP 403).
- The total breeding-pair figures quoted by ERA conflict and are probably species-specific; unverified.
- The "27 occasional breeders" were not named by the source.
- The title and year of the "recently published national Breeding Bird Atlas" (BirdLife, 2026) were not found.

## Which seabirds nest on Filfla, Comino, the Gozo and Malta cliffs?

### Takeaway
Four seabirds breed: **European storm-petrel** (Filfla, the Mediterranean stronghold), **Scopoli's shearwater** (Ta' Ċenċ the largest colony, also Filfla), **Yelkouan shearwater** (Rdum tal-Madonna/L-Aħrax, Comino, Ta' Ċenċ, St Paul's Islands, Majjistral) and **Yellow-legged gull** (mainly Filfla). All nest in cliff holes, caves and boulder screes and visit colonies at night (except the gull). Rats, light pollution and illegal shooting are the main threats.

### Cited Findings
- **Storm-petrel (Kanġu ta' Filfla):** body length 14–16 cm, "not much bigger than a sparrow", wingspan 37–41 cm; "Filfla holds 5,000-8,000 breeding pairs, more than a half of the Mediterranean Storm-petrel subspecies", SB. Breeding season "prolonged – from April till October", SB.
- Storm-petrel subspecies *melitensis* "is the only bird with Malta (melitensis) in its scientific name"; national population "estimated at between 11,000 and 16,000 pairs, with the largest colony on Filfla"; Antonio Schembri named it *Thalassidroma melitensis* in 1843, MBR23 (cover note).
- Storm-petrels breed in low numbers at Ta' Ċenċ, Għarb and L-Aħrax; 600 counted at Filfla on 18 Jul 2023; up to 995 at Qawra in Oct, MBR23. Large offshore flocks gather at fish farms (South-East Aquaculture Zone, 500 birds), MBR22.
- First Mediterranean storm-petrel nestling ringed at L-Irdum tal-Madonna in 2017, [BirdLife Malta 2017](https://birdlifemalta.org/2017/11/first-mediterranean-storm-petrel-breeding-malta/) (title via search).
- Filfla colony was about 10,000 pairs until the mid-1980s, [MaltaToday 2013](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/environment/31258/risk-of-rat-infestation-on-filfla-could-kill-seabird-population-20131106) (via search summary). In 1970 Filfla was "still a stronghold of the Storm Petrel" but was being used as a **bombing target**, which reduced shearwaters and killed storm-petrels, S&G70.
- Yellow-legged gulls prey on storm-petrels on Filfla, [Il-Merill 1995 paper](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/37137/1/Predation_by_the_Yellow-legged_gull_Larus_cachinnans_on_Storm_petrels_Hydrobates_pelagicus_on_Filfla_1995.pdf) (title).
- **Scopoli's shearwater (Ċiefa):** body length 45–52 cm, wingspan 112–122 cm; Maltese population 4,500–5,000 pairs; "Ta' Cenc cliffs in Gozo are home to the largest colony", SB. Seen late Feb to early Nov; rafts of 800 at Ta' Ċenċ and Tas-Safra; colonies at Ta' Ċenċ, Filfla, Miġra l-Ferħa; ringing at Fungus Rock, MBR22; MBR23.
- **Yelkouan shearwater (Garnija):** body length 30–35 cm, wingspan 70–84 cm; returns to colonies in winter; "Each pair lays one egg between the end of February and beginning of March"; colonies "come alive at night with their calls, which sound like a crying baby"; "With 1,660 – 1,980 pairs Malta hosts approximately 10% of the world population. The largest colonies are found at Rdum tal-Madonna, along Malta's south-eastern cliffs, on Comino and Ta' Cenc in Gozo", SB. 2023 colonies: L-Aħrax, Kemmuna, St Paul's Islands, Majjistral Park, MBR23. Older census figures ranged from 495 pairs (1983) to 1,890 (2008), [Borg, Avocetta](https://avocetta.org/down/oq) (via search summary).
- The LIFE+ Malta Seabird Project (Sep 2011 – Jun 2016) mapped marine Important Bird Areas and ran "the first rat control programme at a Yelkouan Shearwater colony"; threats named include light pollution from sea traffic, SB.
- **Yellow-legged gull:** breeds "mainly on Filfla as usual, but also at smaller scattered colonies such as St Paul's Islands and Kemmuna", MBR23. Filfla colony ~150 pairs on the plateau (1987), counted yearly since 1982, via search summary of [Il-Merill 24](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/38531/1/Il-Merill_24_1986-87_1987.pdf) and [BirdLife Filfla gull census blog](https://birdlifemalta.org/2014/04/filfla-yellow-legged-gull-colony-trip).
- In 1970 the cliff breeders were listed as "Shearwaters (*Procellaria diomedia* and *Puffinus puffinus*), the Herring Gull, the Blue Rock Thrush and the odd pair of Kestrels and Peregrines" (old names: the "Manx/*puffinus*" shearwater is today's Yelkouan; the "Herring Gull" is today's Yellow-legged gull); rats along the coasts kept storm-petrels off Malta and Gozo, S&G70.
- Cory's shearwater (*C. borealis*, **Ċiefa tal-Atlantiku**) has only 2 confirmed records (2019, 2022), both among Scopoli's rafts at Ta' Ċenċ, MBR22.

### Inferences
- Colony map for spawning (cliff-face, night only): **Filfla** = storm-petrel + gulls + some Scopoli's; **Ta' Ċenċ (Gozo)** = biggest Scopoli's colony + Yelkouan + some storm-petrels; **Rdum tal-Madonna / L-Aħrax (Mellieħa)** = biggest Yelkouan colony; **Comino** = Yelkouan; **St Paul's Islands** = Yelkouan + gulls; **Majjistral / Miġra l-Ferħa / Dingli–Fawwara cliffs** = shearwaters, gulls at Fawwara/Mtaħleb; **Għarb (Gozo west)** = storm-petrels.
- Game design: shearwaters skim the sea surface in lines by day and raft offshore in the evening before coming in after dark; the Yelkouan's "crying baby" call is a ready-made ambient sound for cliff tops at night. Storm-petrels flutter low over waves "pattering" with their feet (general) and smell of musk (general).
- Filfla is a strict nature reserve (general knowledge; not checked in this pass) and no players should be able to farm its birds in a realistic version.

### Gaps
- No current (2023–2025) Filfla gull count was found.
- Exact current Scopoli's numbers per colony (Ta' Ċenċ vs Filfla) were not found.
- ERA's MSFD seabird assessment refused access (HTTP 403).

## Which are the most common/visible migrants and winter visitors?

### Takeaway
Malta sits on a central-Mediterranean flyway. **Spring passage** peaks March–mid-May (best at Kemmuna and the Comino Channel); **autumn passage** runs from mid-August to November, with **raptor passage** peaking around 20 September (Buskett roost). In **winter** the countryside holds mostly robins, chiffchaffs, blackcaps, stonechats, black redstarts, white wagtails (13,007 at a Valletta roost), meadow pipits and starlings (flocks of 1,000–2,000), with gulls at Salina. About 230 species are recorded in an average year.

### Cited Findings

**Seasons and hotspots**
- "In an average year, around 230 species are recorded in Malta"; 235 species in 2022, MBR22.
- Spring: first trans-Saharan migrants (House Martin, Green Sandpiper) around mid-February; birders watch the **Kemmuna Channel from Ċirkewwa** for flocks of pintail, garganey, ferruginous duck, shoveler, whimbrel, pomarine skua and glossy ibis; **Kemmuna** "can be particularly productive"; in March: spotted crake, marsh harrier, pallid harrier, lesser kestrel, hoopoe, northern and isabelline wheatears, subalpine warbler; in April: bee-eater, whinchat, tree pipit, pied and collared flycatchers, wood warbler, whitethroat, garden warbler; late April: turtle-dove, squacco heron, little bittern, honey-buzzard, red-footed falcon, icterine warbler, spotted flycatcher, golden oriole, MBR22.
- Autumn: raptors from mid-August (Eleonora's falcon, marsh harrier, Montagu's harrier, black kite), plus flocks of night-herons, purple and grey herons; "the most abundant birds of prey ... are by far Western Marsh-harrier, European Honey-buzzard and Eurasian Hobby", frequent osprey, black kite, Montagu's harrier, sparrowhawk, kestrel, lesser kestrel, Eleonora's falcon; "Throughout September, European Bee-eater is very common and fills the air with its calls"; "Soaring-bird migration generally peaks around 20 September"; **Buskett** is the favoured raptor watch site "because the surrounding area attracts several raptors to roost for the night"; flamingo flocks from mid-August with "exhausted juveniles regularly stranding along the coast", MBR22.
- Late autumn: mid-October to November brings skylark, chiffchaff, blackcap, robin, song thrush, stonechat, black redstart and finches; Nov–Dec golden plover, lapwing and woodcock at the Airport and sheltered areas, "highly persecuted"; common cranes "increasingly scarce", MBR22.
- Winter: countryside "rather impoverished of birds during winter" because of hunting, "except for resident species and common wintering species such as Common Chiffchaff, Eurasian Blackcap, European Robin, Common Stonechat, Black Redstart, White Wagtail, Meadow Pipit and Common Starling"; Buskett is "practically the only safe haven" for wintering thrushes and finches (song thrush, chaffinch, some blackbird, redwing, siskin); also small numbers of wryneck, skylark, grey wagtail, dunnock, goldcrest, firecrest; **Salina** draws large numbers of gulls (black-headed, Mediterranean, yellow-legged; also slender-billed, lesser black-backed, Audouin's), plus Sandwich tern, cormorant, kingfisher, black-necked grebe, MBR22.

## Which species are culturally important (hunting, trapping finches: goldfinch, linnet, greenfinch, serin, chaffinch)?

### Takeaway
The **merill** is the national bird (since 1971; on the old Lm1 coin). Hunting and trapping are deep, contested traditions: about 8,000 licensed hunters; **spring hunting of quail and turtle dove**; autumn hunting of 40 land species; legal **clap-net trapping** of song thrush and golden plover; and the long fight over **finch trapping** (chaffinch, linnet, greenfinch, goldfinch, serin, siskin, hawfinch), ruled illegal by the EU Court in 2018 and 2024. Finch keeping and breeding in cages is a widespread pastime; Malta even has its own captive Spanish-sparrow colour forms. Illegal shooting wiped out breeding raptors and barn owls; bird ringing at Għadira and the reserves is the conservation counterpoint.

### Cited Findings

**National bird**
- Blue rock thrush "is Malta's national bird, adopted in 1971", Maltese name "Merill", "appeared on the Lm 1 coins of the former Maltese lira", and "inspired place names, songs, stamps, poems, and sayings about talkativeness"; 21–23 cm; males blue-grey, females brown and scaly; nests in rock cavities and walls, 3–5 eggs, [Wikipedia: Blue rock thrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_rock_thrush)
- BirdLife Malta's journal is called *Il-Merill* (first issue 1970, with the breeding birds paper), S&G70.

**Hunting**
- 2025 spring season: "a total of 7,969 hunters (6,643 in Malta and 1,326 in Gozo)" licensed; autumn 2026 (1 Sep 2026 – 31 Jan 2027) hunting "permitted for 40 species from land and 12 species at sea"; spring quarry Common Quail (Summiena) and European Turtle-dove (Gamiema); bag limits turtle-dove 500 (autumn) and 1,500 (spring), quail 2,400 (spring); poachers target raptors, herons, flamingo, black stork, spoonbill, [BirdLife Malta hunting](https://birdlifemalta.org/information/hunting/)
- Spring 2026: quail 13 Apr – 3 May, turtle dove 20 Apr – 3 May; Ornis Committee rejected a turtle-dove moratorium, [BirdLife Malta, Mar 2026](https://birdlifemalta.org/2026/03/ornis-committee-caves-to-political-pressure-ignores-science-as-turtle-dove-decline-worsens/) and [Komitee gegen den Vogelmord](https://www.komitee.de/en/latest-news/news/malta-green-light-spring-hunting/) (via search summary).
- Woodcock, golden plover and lapwing in Nov–Dec "are highly persecuted and seeing them is often a bittersweet experience"; a buzzard turned up "showing gunshot injuries" (2022), MBR22.
- 1970: "the number of bird shooters and trappers has greatly increased"; quail "is one of the most sought after gamebirds by the local shooters"; nests found by "dogs that follow the farmers and the shooters"; many birds ended "as just another stuffed specimen beneath a glass case" (taxidermy), S&G70.

**Trapping**
- Song Thrush (Malvizz) and Golden Plover (Pluviera) are the only species legally trappable in 2026; finches in the 2026 "research" derogation: Greenfinch (Verdun), Goldfinch (Gardell), Hawfinch (Taż-Żebbuġ), Chaffinch (Sponsun), Serin (Apparell), Linnet (Ġojjin) and Siskin (Ekru), [BirdLife Malta trapping](https://birdlifemalta.org/information/trapping/)
- Method: **clap nets**, "two large nets laid parallel on the ground that swing shut towards each other"; sites cleared of vegetation; **live decoys** in tiny cages or tied by a harness; for finches "a tethered finch swung up and down"; cage traps and electronic callers illegal; lapwing and ruff illegally used as golden-plover decoys, same source.
- Seasons 2026: song thrush 20 Oct – 31 Dec; golden plover 1 Nov – 10 Jan; finches 20 Oct – 20 Dec; quotas 5,000 song thrush and 700 golden plover; 1,800 thrush/plover sites plus 3,688 finch sites (page also gives 1,641; inconsistent), same source.
- Finch-trapping ban was a condition of the 2004 EU Accession Treaty; reintroduced 2014; EU Court ruled against Malta on 21 June 2018 and again 19 September 2024 ("does not establish a genuine research purpose"), [BirdLife trapping](https://birdlifemalta.org/information/trapping/); [BirdLife 2024](https://birdlifemalta.org/2024/09/ecj-ruling-puts-an-end-to-scientific-finch-trapping/)
- "Due to the widespread pastime of keeping and breeding finches in Malta, it is probable that a significant share of sightings in the wild refer to escapees", MBR23. Wild goldfinch, serin, greenfinch and linnet are now seen only in single figures, MBR22.
- 1970: "The Maltese bird-trappers concentrate mainly on finches and so the occasional pair of Serins, Greenfinches or Goldfinches that tries to nest is not given much chance"; linnet still nested round **Xagħra and Nadur** in Gozo; yellow wagtails were netted in hundreds at Ta' Qali in April, S&G70.
- Two Maltese captive forms of the Spanish sparrow, **"Opal" and "Agate" Spanish Sparrow**, were documented as landraces bred by local fanciers (Sciberras, Sciberras & Abela), [GuideMeMalta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/psst-psst-two-unique-maltese-bird-breeds-officially-recognised-in-scientific-study)

**Ringing and reserves**
- BirdLife Malta runs the **Valletta Bird Ringing Scheme** (EURING member); main ringing sites 2000–2005: Għadira, Simar, Rabat, Buskett, Salina, Lunzjata Valley; Għadira and Simar run as constant-effort sites; earliest report covers 1979–81, [Ringing report 2000–2005](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/36475/1/Ringing_report_for_2000-2005_2010.pdf), [BirdLife bird ringing](https://birdlifemalta.org/conservation/bird-ringing/) (via search summary). Għadira was proposed as a national nature reserve in 1978 (Axell's 1980 plan), [Centro 1990](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/37620/1/Centro_vol_1_no_5_1990.pdf) (via search summary).
- In 1970 BirdLife's forerunner (Malta Ornithological Society) campaigned to make Għadira "the only such stretch of fresh water" a sanctuary, and had Comino declared a bird sanctuary, S&G70.
- Ringing in practice: 691 birds ringed across sites on 1 May 2022, 347 on Kemmuna, MBR22. Ringed birds from Finland, Norway, Hungary, Czechia recovered, MBR22.

**Former breeders and losses (history)**
- **Jackdaw (Ċawla):** "used to breed commonly is now extinct. The last breeding pairs were exterminated in Malta in 1945 and in Gozo, where they had the last hold on the N.E. cliffs of Xagħra, in 195[?]" (year partly illegible in the scan), S&G70.
- **Barn owl (Barbaġann):** lost as a breeder in the 1980s to illegal hunting; reintroduction by the hunters' federation FKNK from 2018 at a Buskett breeding facility (€80,000 from the Conservation of Wild Birds Fund); released birds shot (one found at Dwejra on 7 Feb 2026, the second since Nov 2025); species "remains locally extinct"; BirdLife lists Jackdaw, Kestrel, Peregrine and Barn-owl as once-common breeders lost, [BirdLife 2026](https://birdlifemalta.org/2026/02/another-barn-owl-from-reintroduction-project-shot-as-species-remains-locally-extinct/). In 1970 a few pairs still bred on inland cliffs and valley sides of Gozo, S&G70. Barn owl singles at Sannat 2023 possibly escapees, MBR23.
- **Peregrine (Bies):** "very scarce" in 1970, S&G70; last pair reportedly shot at Ta' Ċenċ in 1982, return since 2009, breeding pair 2015, via search summary ([pipedot](https://pipedot.org/PKX4), [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/93160/watch_barn_owls_set_to_return_to_the_maltese_wild_parliamentary_secretary_says)), **unverified**; bred again on southern cliffs 2019 (BB19) and 2022 (MBR22).
- **Kestrel, Woodchat Shrike:** "very scarce" breeders in 1970, S&G70; kestrel now a few pairs again (MBR22/23); woodchat now only a migrant (MBR22).
- **Corn Bunting (Durrajsa):** common nester in open spaces in 1970, S&G70; 2022: "can probably be declared extinct as a local breeding bird", MBR22; "Formerly a regular breeding species ... seldom seen", MBR23.
- **Rock Dove (Tudun tal-Ġebel):** 1970: "should not figure as a breeding bird, as the few pairs that still breed on the south western cliffs of Malta and Gozo are not of a purely wild strain owing to successive interbreeding with domestic feral pigeons", S&G70.
- **Linnet, Serin, Greenfinch, Goldfinch, Chaffinch, Quail:** sporadic breeders in 1970, held back by trapping, S&G70.
- **Sardinian warbler** became a common breeder between 1874 and 1885; **Tree sparrow** first nests in Gozo 1967, S&G70.

**Introduced / feral**
- Chukar on Kemmuna: "Introduced and established species (i.e. Category C)", MBR22.
- Feral Little Egret (~15) and Cattle Egret (up to 236) around Salina, Simar and Għadira, MBR22; MBR23.
- Monk parakeet: Malta appears in a general list of places with self-sustaining feral monk parakeet populations (no numbers), [Wikipedia mirror, Monk parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Monk_Parakeet) (via search summary only). Monk parakeet is **not** on the official Malta checklist (C) and is not in MBR22/MBR23.

### Inferences
- Cultural set for the mod: **merill** (national bird; could be the mod's "signature" bird and a cliff/rooftop spawn), **goldfinch, linnet, greenfinch, serin, chaffinch, siskin, hawfinch** (the trapped "song finches"; cage-bird culture, trapping sites in fields), **song thrush and golden plover** (clap-net quarry), **quail and turtle dove** (spring hunting), **storm-petrel *melitensis*** (the bird named after Malta), **barn owl** (lost and being brought back at Buskett), **Spanish sparrow** (the everyday town bird; Opal/Agate captive forms).
- Game design caution: hunting and trapping are politically sensitive in Malta; a neutral design (e.g. bird ringing / birdwatching with BirdLife-style ringing stations at Għadira, Simar, Buskett, Kemmuna) fits the real conservation story without endorsing illegal activity. All wild birds are protected under the EU Birds Directive except listed quarry species in their seasons (general; the full autumn quarry list of 40 species was not retrieved).
- Finch notes **(general)**: goldfinch = red face, gold wing bars, tinkling song (the prized cage bird); linnet = male with crimson breast and forehead, twittering; greenfinch = olive-green with yellow wing flashes, wheezing "dzweee"; serin = tiny yellow-streaked, fast jingling song; chaffinch = male pink breast, blue-grey cap, "pink-pink" call.

## Notable vagrants (short) and the checklist in numbers

### Takeaway
The official checklist holds **408 species** (Jan 2026), but only about 230 occur in a typical year; most of the rest are rare vagrants that are not worth individual mobs.

### Cited Findings
- 408 numbered species on the Malta Bird Checklist, Malta Rarities and Records Committee, updated Jan 2026, C.
- Examples of rarities recorded 2022–2023: Egyptian Nightjar (11th record), Eyebrowed Thrush, Terek Sandpiper, Swinhoe's Storm-petrel, Cory's Shearwater (2nd), Moussier's Redstart, Desert Wheatear, Trumpeter Finch, Iberian Chiffchaff (4th), Razorbill influxes, Mute Swan (Gozo, died), Common Scoter, Dartford Warbler (Majjistral), Hen Harrier (Kemmuna), Northern Wren, MBR22; MBR23.
- Further rare species on the checklist include Black Stork, White Stork, Egyptian Vulture, Griffon Vulture, Eastern Imperial Eagle, Golden Eagle, White-tailed Sea-eagle, Great Bustard, Little Bustard, African Houbara, Cream-coloured Courser, Atlantic Puffin, Little Auk, Wallcreeper, Bohemian Waxwing, White-winged Snowfinch, Alpine Accentor, plus American strays such as Red-eyed Vireo, Rose-breasted Grosbeak, Indigo Bunting and Blue-winged Teal, C.

- If the mod wants a single "rare bird" easter egg, the flamingo (regular but erratic), black stork or a vulture fit a real Malta record and read well in-game.

### Gaps
