# Land and freshwater invertebrates of the Maltese Islands (for a Maltese bugs content mod)

Researched 2026-11-14. Sources were fetched and read where possible. Several University of Malta PDFs were downloaded and read in full as text: the 2018 Cassar butterfly revision, the 2012 Corso et al. Odonata checklist, the 2020 Cassar mantis paper, Schembri's "Malta's unique land snails" and Mifsud's "Insects of Malta". Maltese names are given **only where a source spells them**. Common Maltese words the brief supplied but that no source here confirmed are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Group totals: how big is the Maltese invertebrate fauna?

### Takeaway
Malta has about 4,500 recorded terrestrial and freshwater arthropod species, and an estimated 6,000–8,000 insect species in all. Beetles are the largest group (more than 1,700). About 600 of the Lepidoptera are moths, against only about 30 butterflies with a natural occurrence. There are about 150 spiders and about 77 non-marine snails and slugs.

### Cited Findings
- **Insects (estimate):** the Entomological Society of Malta estimates 6,000–8,000 insect species in Malta. Since 2008 its Bulletin has covered more than 1,200 insects, about a quarter of them new records for Malta. More than 300 species had lived in Malta for centuries unrecorded., [Mifsud, "Insects of Malta", THINK magazine (UM)](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- **Arthropods (recorded):** "an estimated 4,500 species of terrestrial and freshwater arthropods" (Dandria & Mifsud 2017)., [Cassar 2020, Praying mantises of the Maltese Islands, Fragmenta entomologica](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/fragmenta_entomologica/article/download/462/436)
- **Counts by order:**
  - Beetles (Coleoptera): "more than 1,700";
  - Lepidoptera: "some two dozen butterflies and some 600 species of moths";
  - Hymenoptera: "might be over 2,000", largely ignored;
  - flies (Diptera): 600 identified, possibly 1,000 present;
  - Hemiptera (bugs, whiteflies, cicadas): about 600 found.

 , [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- **Beetle families (Mifsud's reviews):**
  - weevils (Curculionoidea): 182 species;
  - bark beetles: 23;
  - scarabs (Scarabaeoidea): 54;
  - Pselaphinae: 14.

 , [UM OAR, Scarabaeoidea of the Maltese Islands](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/529/1/The%20Scarabaeoidea%20of%20the%20Maltese%20Islands.pdf); [Pselaphinae Malta, Sabella & Mifsud](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/12423/1/Pselaphinae%20Malta%20-%20Sabella%20%26%20Mifsud.pdf); counts as summarised in search results, papers not opened
- **Spiders:**
  - 137 species in 31 families, including 7 endemics (Dandria, Falzon & Henwood 2005)., [CMN 4(2) 2005](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/15124/1/CMN-4-2-2005-A2.pdf)
  - 141 species in 2018., [Biodiversity Journal 9(1) 2018](https://www.biodiversityjournal.com/pdf/9(1)_95-98.pdf)
  - 150 species from 31 families (Rehfeldt & Cassar)., [Arachnologische Mitteilungen 67](https://www.zobodat.at/pdf/Arachnologische-Mitteilungen_67_0018-0047.pdf)

  (The counts are from search summaries; the papers were not opened.)
- **Non-marine molluscs:**
  - 15 freshwater gastropods;
  - 62 land gastropods (77 non-marine gastropods in all);
  - 2 freshwater bivalves (*Pisidium*), giving 79 non-marine molluscs.

  The page's introduction says 78 gastropods, so it contradicts itself., [Wikipedia, List of non-marine molluscs of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_non-marine_molluscs_of_Malta)
- **Land snails:** "of the 60 or so species known … some 20% are endemic"., [Schembri, "Malta's unique land snails" (UM OAR)](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/42276/1/Malta%27s%20unique%20land%20snails.pdf)
- **Odonata:** 18 recorded species on the Maltese islands; 15 of the 18 recorded in the whole study area (the Sicilian Channel islands) are breeding species., [Corso et al. 2012, Biodiversity Journal 3(4): 459–478](https://www.biodiversityjournal.com/pdf/3(4)_459-478.pdf)
- **Butterflies:** 64 species have been listed for Malta between 1858 and 2017. Of these, 30 are resident, migrant, naturalised or vagrant; the other 34 are erroneous, dubious or brought in by people., [Cassar 2018, Naturalista sicil. XLII: 3–19](https://sssn.it/PDF/PDF%20NS%2042/3-19.pdf)
- **Endemism:** a blog citing Schembri (1994) gives 55 endemic animals, 81% of them arthropods. Schembri (2003) warns that some "endemics" have since been found elsewhere., search summary of [muticaria.blogspot (Schembri)](https://muticaria.blogspot.com/2009/06/part-ii-endemic-species-of-maltese.html); secondary source

### Inferences
- For a game, about 30 butterflies, 18 dragonflies, 3 mantises, 1 scorpion and about 13 endemic snails are small enough to model in full. Beetles, moths, flies and spiders need a short list of notable species.

### Gaps
- No consolidated total of endemic insects or endemic beetles was found.
- Centipedes, millipedes, woodlice, ants, Orthoptera and earwigs: no species counts were found.

## 2. Butterflies (full list)

### Takeaway
The authoritative modern list (Cassar 2018) has **30 species** that occur naturally. Several familiar names in older lists are wrong:
- the local "Bath White" is *Pontia edusa*;
- the "Common Blue" is *Polyommatus celina*;
- the grass jewel / tiny blue is *Zizeeria karsandra*;
- the Scarce Swallowtail is dubious.

The **Maltese Swallowtail** *Papilio machaon melitensis* is the flagship endemic subspecies. The "Maltese/Mediterranean skipper" of the brief is *Gegenes pumilio* (Pigmy Skipper), Malta's only skipper.

### Cited Findings
All statuses are from [Cassar 2018](https://sssn.it/PDF/PDF%20NS%2042/3-19.pdf) unless another source is given.

## 3. Moths (notable ones)

### Takeaway
There are about 600 moth species. The notable ones are:
- 7 hawk-moths, including the Death's-head, Oleander, Hummingbird and Convolvulus Hawk-moths and *Hyles sammuti*;
- the endemic Maltese Ruby Tiger subspecies;
- two recently described endemic micro-moths.

### Cited Findings
- **Hawk-moths (Sphingidae) on the Maltese list:**
  - Death's-head *Acherontia atropos*;
  - Convolvulus *Agrius convolvuli*;
  - Oleander *Daphnis nerii*;
  - Silver-striped *Hippotion celerio*;
  - Striped *Hyles livornica*;
  - *Hyles sammuti* Eitschberger, Danner & Surholt 1998;
  - Hummingbird *Macroglossum stellatarum*.

 , [Wikipedia, List of Lepidoptera of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lepidoptera_of_Malta)
- **Tiger and footman moths (Erebidae) on the same list:** Crimson-speckled *Utetheisa pulchella*, Ruby Tiger *Phragmatobia fuliginosa*, *Cymbalophora pudica*, *Coscinia striata*, *Dysauxes famula* and footmen (*Eilema*, *Lithosia quadra*). The subfamily grouping is mine, not the page's., [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lepidoptera_of_Malta)
- **Endemic subspecies:** the Maltese Ruby Tiger *Phragmatobia fuliginosa* ssp. *melitensis*., [Wikipedia, Endemic Maltese wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_Maltese_wildlife)
- **Endemic micro-moths (recently described):** the bagworm moth *Stichobasis postmeridianus* and the fungus moth *Eudarcia melitensis* (Tineidae)., [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- **Names honouring Malta or Maltese naturalists**, not labelled endemic on the source page: *Epidola melitensis*, *Agdistis melitensis*, *Sciopetris melitensis*, *Xylena exsoleta maltensis*, *Cydia sammuti*, *Aporophyla chioleuca sammuti*., [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lepidoptera_of_Malta)
- A Maltese nature blog has a post on the Red Underwing (*Catocala* sp.)., [Natural Malta blog](https://naturalmalta.blogspot.com/2013/03/the-cicada.html) (post title only)

### Inferences
- **Game candidates:**
  - Hummingbird Hawk-moth: day-flying, hovers at flowers;
  - Death's-head Hawk-moth: rare and large, skull pattern on the thorax, squeaks; a well-known general fact, not sourced here;
  - Oleander Hawk-moth: green camouflage; larvae on oleander in gardens;
  - Maltese Ruby Tiger.

### Gaps
- The endemic status of *Hyles sammuti* and the various *melitensis* moths was not confirmed.
- No Maltese names for moths were found.
- Hawk-moth seasons and abundance in Malta were not found.

## 5. Bees and wasps

### Takeaway
The **Maltese honey bee** *Apis mellifera ruttneri* (described in 1997, related to the North African *A. m. intermissa*) is protected as Malta's national insect, but heavily hybridised. The **oriental hornet** *Vespa orientalis* is native, but booming and preying on hives. The **large carpenter bee** *Xylocopa violacea* is **il-Bomblu l-iswed**.

### Cited Findings
**Maltese honey bee**
- Described 1997 (Sheppard et al.). Its mtDNA and behaviour put it closer to North African *A. m. intermissa*., [Apidologie 1997](https://www.apidologie.org/10.1051/apido:19970505)
- A 2017 study found 32 colonies matching the original morphometrics, after Varroa losses and imported queens., [UM OAR 22852](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/22852)
- 2025: only about 11% of sampled bees showed no introgression from other subspecies (13% in 2023); it faces a high risk of extinction; it is legally protected as the national insect, but queen imports remain a threat., [BusinessNow.mt](https://businessnow.mt/maltas-endemic-honey-bee-faces-high-risk-of-extinction-new-research-warns/); [Journal of Apicultural Science 2025](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/jas-2025-0008) (search summaries)
- Compared with imported *A. m. ligustica* in spring, the Maltese bee had more adults, brood and pollen, but swarmed more and was less calm on the comb., search summary of [UM OAR 67597](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/67597)
- **Melite:** Malta's ancient name Μελίτη (Melitē) is linked to "honey-sweet"., [Wikipedia, Maltese honey bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_honey_bee) (a weak source on etymology)
- The conservation NGO's site is called **nahlamaltija.org** ("Naħla Maltija" = Maltese bee)., [nahlamaltija.org](https://nahlamaltija.org/2022/11/29/megachile-sculpturalis-hi-nahla-aljena-u-invaziva-fl-ewropa/)
- Honey is "a delicacy in Malta and Gozo"., [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)

**Oriental hornet** (*Vespa orientalis*)
- Maltese name reported as **Bagħal taż-Żunżan / Żunżan Bagħal** (search result summary; spelling to verify).
- Reddish-brown with yellow bands on the head and abdomen.
- **Native** to Malta, but its numbers have boomed and it now comes into towns.
- Active from late April to the second week of November (2019 study); queens appear first, then workers.
- Preys on honey bees. One trade report says 70% of hives were destroyed (unverified).
- "Very unlikely to attack people" if not provoked; attracted to meat and pet food.

- [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/105231/invasion_alert_aggressive_oriential_hornets_spread_in_malta_is_growing); [GuideMe Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/bee-on-the-lookout-oriental-hornet-invasion-is-growing-on-the-maltese-islands); [UM OAR 96311](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/96311); [Bee Culture](https://www.beeculture.com/oriental-hornets-destroy-70-of-colonies/); [Lovin Malta](https://lovinmalta.com/lifestyle/pets/lock-your-meat-malta-carnivorous-oriental-hornets-keep-getting-attracted-to-cat-food-and-stinging-pets) (search summaries)

**Other bees and wasps**
- **Large carpenter bee** *Xylocopa violacea*: Maltese **il-Bomblu l-iswed** ("the black bumble"); recorded at Chadwick Lakes., [Chadwick Lakes biodiversity](https://chadwicklakes.mt/biodiversity/large-carpenter-bee-xylocopa-violacea-il-bomblu-l-iswed/)
- Mantis egg cases in Malta are parasitised by the torymid wasp *Podagrion splendens*., [Cassar 2020](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/fragmenta_entomologica/article/download/462/436)

### Inferences
- Game ideas:
  - wild bee nests in valley cliffs;
  - beekeeping of the dark Maltese bee;
  - hornets as a hostile mob that raids hives (late April to November) and as a summer pest at food.

### Gaps
- Paper wasps (*Polistes*) and mason bees (*Osmia*, *Megachile*, *Chalicodoma*) in Malta were not researched for lack of time; there is no sourced Maltese species list.
- The history of beekeeping in Malta (Roman-era apiaries, "Melite") was not sourced beyond the Wikipedia etymology.

## 6. Beetles

### Takeaway
There are more than 1,700 species. Notable ones:
- endemics: the darkling beetle *Pimelia rugulosa melitana* (Maltese field beetle), *Microptinus melitensis* and *Amaurops mifsudi*;
- the Seven-spot Ladybird (**Nannakola tas-Seba' Tikki**);
- serious invasive pests: the Red Palm Weevil (palms), the African longhorn *Phryneta leprosa* (mulberries) and the bark beetle *Hypocryphalus scabricollis* (figs).

### Cited Findings
- **Maltese field beetle** *Pimelia rugulosa* ssp. *melitana*: endemic., [Wikipedia, Endemic Maltese wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_Maltese_wildlife)
- *Microptinus melitensis*: endemic to Malta. *Amaurops mifsudi* (Pselaphinae): described as endemic in 1999. *Opatrum melitense* (darkling beetle): presumed endemic, not verified., search summaries of [CMN 3(2) 2000](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar//bitstream/123456789/14937/1/CMN-3-2-2000-A1.pdf), [Pselaphinae paper](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/12423/1/Pselaphinae%20Malta%20-%20Sabella%20%26%20Mifsud.pdf), [Tenebrionidae of Malta](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/539/1/Faunistic%20and%20taxonomic%20updates%20on%20the%20Tenebrionidae%20of%20Malta.pdf)
- **Seven-spot Ladybird** *Coccinella septempunctata*: Maltese **Nannakola tas-Seba' Tikki**; at Chadwick Lakes., [Chadwick Lakes biodiversity](https://chadwicklakes.mt/biodiversity/)
- **Invasive pests:**
  - Red Palm Weevil: still killing many palms, thousands of euros of damage;
  - *Phryneta leprosa* (longhorn): from Central Africa in timber, has killed hundreds of black mulberry trees;
  - *Hypocryphalus scabricollis* (Asian bark beetle): killing fig trees across Malta, probably arrived on imported *Ficus microcarpa*. Malta has about 10 fig species.

 , [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- A Maltese blog has a post on "The amusing dung beetle"., [Natural Malta](https://naturalmalta.blogspot.com/2013/03/the-cicada.html) (title only)

### Inferences
- *Pimelia* (large, black, flightless, walks on open ground) suits an ambient ground mob. The Red Palm Weevil fits a "palm dies" world event.

### Gaps
- Stag beetles, rhinoceros beetles (*Oryctes*), oil beetles, fireflies and ground beetles in Malta: not found in sources this session. Do not add them without checking the Scarabaeoidea review (54 species) and other Mifsud papers.
- No consolidated list of endemic beetles was found. Maltese names for beetles in general (e.g. "ħanfusa") were not confirmed.

## 7. Mantises, cicadas, Orthoptera and other insects

### Takeaway
There are **three mantises**, all called **Debba tax-Xitan** ("devil's mare"; older form Debba tal-Infern):
- *Mantis religiosa*, common;
- *Ameles spallanzania*, very common and small;
- *Rivetina baetica*, rare, in garrigue.

The Maltese cicada (**weżieq ta' bi nhar**) has recently been shown to be *Cicada barbara*, not *C. orni*. The Asian tiger mosquito arrived in 2009.

### Cited Findings
All mantis findings are from [Cassar 2020, Fragmenta entomologica](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/fragmenta_entomologica/article/download/462/436).

**Mantises**
- Three species are known: *Ameles spallanzania*, *Mantis religiosa* and *Rivetina baetica*. *Ameles decolor* is unconfirmed, and Gulia's 1858 *Iris oratoria* and *Blepharopsis mendica* are doubtful.
- Vernacular names: "both Debba tal-Infern and Debba tax-Xitan are used … the latter being more common nowadays" ("Hell's mare" / "Devil's mare").
- ***Mantis religiosa*:**
  - "very common and widespread": garrigue, grassland, clay steppe, field margins, gardens;
  - the only Maltese species with a coxal eyespot;
  - ootheca up to 30 mm, on the walls of buildings;
  - recorded from Malta (e.g. Għargħur, Rabat, Żebbuġ, Binġemma, Birkirkara, Marfa), Gozo (Ta' Ċenċ) and Comino.
- ***Rivetina baetica*:**
  - rare; never recorded from Gozo; known from Comino (St Marija Bay, Blue Lagoon);
  - on Malta mainly on the north and north-east coast (Marfa, Mellieħa, Baħar iċ-Ċagħaq, Selmun, Mistra, Pembroke, Naxxar), with some records from Baħrija, Rabat and Lippija (Mġarr);
  - lives in large unbroken garrigue with terra rossa pockets;
  - males fly to light traps;
  - the female digs a pit to lay its ootheca (about 20 mm) in the soil.
- ***Ameles spallanzania*:**
  - "very common and widespread": garrigue, maquis, clay steppe, field margins, gardens, roadside plantings;
  - recorded from Malta and Gozo (Qbajjar, Rabat);
  - ootheca about 10 mm;
  - adult females roam open ground and bare rock;
  - eats flies and small grasshoppers such as *Pyrgomorpha conica*.

**Cicada**
- Maltese name **weżieq ta' bi nhar**. Males sing through their summer; heard wherever there are trees, including towns; most active in the hottest hours., [Natural Malta, The cicada](https://naturalmalta.blogspot.com/2013/03/the-cicada.html)
- A 2020/2021 acoustic study found the Maltese cicada is *Cicada barbara* (the photo caption gives *C. barbara lusitanica*), not *C. orni*., [UM Newspoint 2021](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/2021/03/cicadas-entomology); [Acoustic analysis of Malta's cicadas](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/68245/1/Acousitc%20analysis%20of%20Malta%e2%80%99s%20cicadas.pdf) (search summaries)

**Other insects**
- **Grasshoppers:** *Pyrgomorpha conica* is a small local grasshopper (prey of *Ameles*)., [Cassar 2020](https://rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa02/fragmenta_entomologica/article/download/462/436). A blog post covers "The slant-faced grasshopper" (*Acrida*?)., [Natural Malta](https://naturalmalta.blogspot.com/2013/03/the-cicada.html) (title only)
- **Cockroaches:** Maltese **wirdien** (plural)., [Malta Daily, "ever wondered what wirdien are called around the world"](https://maltadaily.mt/ever-wondered-what-wirdien-are-called-around-the-world) (headline)
- **Asian tiger mosquito** (*Aedes albopictus*): first reported in Malta in 2009; breeds in any still water, including old tyres., [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- **Endemic flies and whiteflies:**
  - Schembri's spider fly *Ogcodes schembrii* (endemic)., [Wikipedia, Endemic Maltese wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_Maltese_wildlife)
  - The whitefly *Aleurolobus teucrii* was described from Gozo in 1996 (later found in southern Italy)., [Mifsud, THINK](https://thinkmagazine.mt/insects-of-malta/)
- **Book:** *Insects of the Maltese Islands* (Mifsud & Cassar) has recently been published., [THINK46 Insects (UM)](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/135451/1/THINK46-Insects.pdf) (search summary)

### Inferences
- The cicada's summer daytime song is a natural ambient sound for trees (carob, pine) in July and August.
- The mantis suits a passive-hostile-to-insects mob that lays egg cases on walls.

### Gaps
- No sourced information this session on:
  - the Egyptian locust (*Anacridium aegyptium*);
  - crickets (incl. il-grillu: a blog label was seen but not read);
  - ants (nemla);
  - termites;
  - cockroach species;
  - earwigs;
  - stick insects.
- Nothing was found on whether stick insects occur in Malta at all.

## 8. Spiders, scorpions and other arachnids

### Takeaway
Malta has about 150 spider species, including several endemics, notably the trapdoor spiders *Nemesia arboricola* and two *Nemesia* species described in 2022.

There is one native scorpion, *Euscorpius sicanus* (**skorpjun**). It is small (about 5 cm) and practically harmless, and its harmlessness was noted as early as 1536.

Medically relevant spiders: *Steatoda paykulliana* (no bites recorded) and the recluse *Loxosceles rufescens* (one bite case, 2002). No source confirmed a black widow (*Latrodectus*) in Malta.

### Cited Findings
**Spiders**
- The first endemic spider, the trapdoor spider *Nemesia arboricola* Pocock 1903, was not re-found for a century. A 2022 study described two further *Nemesia* species from the archipelago., search summary of [CMN 2005](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/15124/1/CMN-4-2-2005-A2.pdf) and [recentlyextinctspecies](https://recentlyextinctspecies.com/chelicerata/nemesia-arboricola)
- *Menemerus fagei* is the 20th jumping spider for Malta and a new European record., [Arachnologische Mitteilungen 67](https://bioone.org/journals/arachnologische-mitteilungen/volume-67/issue-1/aramit6704/A-contribution-to-the-arachnofauna-Arachnida--Araneae-Opiliones-of/10.30963/aramit6704.full) (search summary)
- *Steatoda paykulliana* (beady spider) is "poisonous to humans", but no bites are reported in Malta. *Loxosceles rufescens* (recluse) caused one case of bite poisoning (Dandria & Mahoney 2002), the first in Malta. Six of the 11 arachnid orders occur in Malta., [UM, Arachnida page](https://staff.um.edu.mt/csav1/srg/arachnida.html)

**Endemic arachnids**
- the **Maltese palpigrade** *Eukoenia christiani* (tiny, soil or caves);
- **Esther's gecko mite** *Gekobia estherae*.

- [Wikipedia, Endemic Maltese wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_Maltese_wildlife)

**Scorpion: *Euscorpius sicanus*, Maltese "Skorpjun"**
- the only native scorpion; about 50 mm;
- nocturnal; hides under stones and logs and in damp cellars; hunts small arthropods in leaf litter;
- extremely mild venom, the sting barely pierces skin;
- Jean Quintin (1536) called them "innocuous in the hands of boys".
-, [GuideMe Malta 2025](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/spotting-the-skorpjun-malta-s-only-native-scorpion-glimpsed-in-the-wild)
- Formerly *E. carpathicus candiota*, from Malta, Gozo, Comino and St Paul's Islands; raised to *E. sicanus* in 2003. Some British Museum "Malta" specimens of *Mesobuthus gibbosus* are probably mislabelled., search summaries of [Fet et al. 2003](https://research.amnh.org/users/lorenzo/PDF/Fet.2003.RSZ.Euscorpius.pdf) and [CMN 2(1) 1990](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/14566/1/CMN-2-1-1990-A4.pdf)
- The UM page says the scorpion has a mild venom and that no stings are reported in Malta., [UM, Arachnida page](https://staff.um.edu.mt/csav1/srg/arachnida.html)

### Inferences
- Endemic trapdoor spiders (*Nemesia*) suit a trapdoor-ambush mob in garrigue or wooded valleys. The scorpion should be a weak, mostly harmless night mob under stones.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed for Malta in any source here:
  - the Mediterranean black widow (*Latrodectus tredecimguttatus*);
  - the false widow (*Steatoda nobilis*/*grossa*);
  - specific wolf spiders (Lycosidae).
- A full list of the endemic spiders is still missing.
- No Maltese word for spider was confirmed ("brimba" is from the brief).

## 10. Land and freshwater snails and slugs

### Takeaway
There are 62 land and 15 freshwater gastropods.

Endemics include:
- the door snails (6 of 8 Clausiliidae endemic): *Lampedusa melitensis* (only at Rdum Dikkiena), *L. gattoi* (Filfla only), *L. mamotica* (Gozo only);
- *Muticaria macrostoma* subspecies;
- *Trochoidea spratti*, *schembrii*, *ogygiaca*, *calcarata*, *cucullus* and *despotti*;
- *Xerocrassa gharlapsi*;
- *Murella* (*Marmorana*) *melitensis* (Bebbuxa tal-Bejt);
- *Cernuella caruanae* (Bebbuxa tat-Torok);
- the slugs *Lehmannia melitensis* and *Deroceras golcheri* (only at il-Maqluba).

The edible garden snail *Cornu aspersum* is **Għakruxa**.

### Cited Findings
**From [Schembri, "Malta's unique land snails"](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/42276/1/Malta%27s%20unique%20land%20snails.pdf)**
- **Door snails (Clausiliidae):** 6 of the 8 species are endemic.
  - *Lampedusa gattoi* lives only on **Filfla**.
  - *Lampedusa mamotica* (Gulia 1861, named after Giuseppe Mamo) lives only in **Gozo**, in a single valley.
  - Four endemics live on Malta, each in a narrow area; *Lampedusa melitensis* (Caruana Gatto) occurs "only in the **Rdum Dikkiena** area".
- **Helicids:** about 9 of about 23 species are endemic.
  - *Trochoidea ogygiaca*: only in Gozo.
  - *T. schembrii*: Gozo, Comino, Cominotto and St Paul's Islands.
  - *T. spratti*: Gozo, Comino and St Paul's Islands.
  - *Marmorana melitensis*, Maltese **"Bebbuxa tal-Bejt"** or **"Bebbuxa tal-Kalli"**, and *Cernuella caruanae*, **"Bebbuxa tat-Torok"**: common all over the islands.
  - Helicids seal the shell mouth with tough membranes in drought.
- **Edible common snail** *Helix aspersa* (= *Cornu aspersum*): Maltese **"Għakruxa"**.
- **Round-mouthed Snail** *Pomatias sulcatus* (endemic race *melitense*): Maltese **"Bebbuxa tal-Fossa"**; common.
- **Blind snails (*Cecilioides*):** 3 of 6 thought endemic; about 5 mm, transparent, live in soil; none seen alive since the 1890s.
- **Slugs:** 2 of 6 are endemic. *Lehmannia melitensis* is known from a few localities in Malta; *Deroceras golcheri* (1962) only from **il-Maqluba near Qrendi**.
- "Few of the endemic Maltese land snails are at all common, the majority are rare and some are very rare"; some have not been found alive since the 1930s.

**Conflicts and checklists**
- *T. spratti* range conflict: Thake (1984) found it absent from Malta, on Comino and Gozo (doubtful St Paul's record), and thought it excludes *T. schembrii*., [CMN 1(3) 1984](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/14539/1/CMN-1-3-1984-A7.pdf) (search summary)
- **Checklist endemics:**
  - *Lampedusa imitatrix* (both subspecies; this is the modern name under which *L. melitensis* is often treated as a subspecies);
  - *Muticaria macrostoma* (some subspecies);
  - *Trochoidea calcarata*, *cucullus*, *despotti*, *ogygiaca*, *schembrii* and *spratti*;
  - *Xerocrassa gharlapsi*;
  - *Murella melitensis*;
  - *Cernuella caruanae* (Malta and Sicily).
- **Introduced:** *Pomatias elegans*, *Discus rotundatus*, *Ambigolimax valentianus*.
- **Other common land snails:** *Theba pisana*, *Eobania vermiculata*, *Cantareus aperta*, *Rumina decollata*, *Cochlicella acuta*.

- [Wikipedia, List of non-marine molluscs of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_non-marine_molluscs_of_Malta)

**Freshwater snails**, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_non-marine_molluscs_of_Malta)
- *Pseudamnicola moussoni*, *Mercuria similis*, *Galba truncatula*, *Planorbis moquini*, *Gyraulus laevis*, *Ancylus fluviatilis* (river limpet), *Heleobia stagnorum*, *Hydrobia* spp.;
- non-native: *Physella acuta* and *Helisoma duryi*;
- probably locally extinct: *Melanoides tuberculata*, *Radix peregra* and *Planorbis planorbis*.

### Inferences
- **Gameplay ideas:**
  - snails as rain-triggered spawns on garrigue and rubble walls;
  - a rare "endemic shell" collectible per island (Filfla door snail, Gozo-only *T. ogygiaca*, Rdum Dikkiena door snail);
  - Għakruxa as an edible item.

### Gaps
- Maltese snail dishes (e.g. bebbux cooked with garlic and herbs, Mġarr / festa snack) were not sourced. The general Maltese word "bebbuxu/bebbux" is attested only through the "Bebbuxa tal-…" names above.
- Exact sizes of the endemic shells were not found.

## 11. Freshwater invertebrates (crab, crayfish, others)

### Takeaway
The **qabru**, the Maltese freshwater crab *Potamon fluviatile lanfrancoi*:
- is an endemic subspecies and Malta's only endemic decapod;
- has been the **National Invertebrate** since 2018 and is legally protected;
- lives only at a few perennial springs in western Malta and in one short Gozo valley;
- probably arrived during the Last Glacial Maximum;
- is bred in captivity by Proġett Qabru.

The invasive **red swamp crayfish** is in Chadwick Lakes.

### Cited Findings
- **Status and protection:** qabru, *Potamon fluviatile lanfrancoi*, "endemic sub-species", endangered. Declared the National Invertebrate in 2018 and legally protected. Proġett Qabru (Nature Trust–FEE with the Aquaculture Directorate, MAFA; sponsored by the HSBC Foundation; authorised by ERA) breeds it in captivity as ex-situ genetic stock for Malta and Gozo., [Aquatic Resources Malta, Progett Qabru](https://aquaticresources.gov.mt/progett-qabru/)
- **ERA:** the only endemic decapod; limited to perennial springs; protected under S.L. 549.44., [ERA national species](https://era.org.mt/topic/national-species/) (search summary; the page refused the fetch, 403)
- **Range:** on Malta it is rare, at a few western sites; Gozo has one population in a short valley. A 2020 study found no genetic connection between valleys., [UM OAR 93746](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/93746) (search summary)
- **Origin:** genetics suggest arrival during the Last Glacial Maximum; human introduction "highly unlikely"., [UM Newspoint 2017](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/features/2017/11/geneticstudyshedslightonthearrivaloffreshwatercrabqabrutomalta)
- **Red swamp crayfish** *Procambarus clarkii*: Maltese **iċ-Ċkala l-ħamra tal-Ilma ħelu**; at Chadwick Lakes (invasive)., [Chadwick Lakes biodiversity](https://chadwicklakes.mt/biodiversity/red-swamp-crayfish-procambarus-clarkii-ic-ckala-l-hamra-tal-ilma-helu/)
- **Freshwater snails:** see section 10, including the river limpet *Ancylus fluviatilis* and *Pisidium* pea clams.

### Inferences
- The qabru suits a rare, protected (non-killable or penalised) crab in spring-fed valleys of western Malta (Baħrija valley area) and in Wied il-Lunzjata (Gozo). These named sites are commonly cited for it but were not confirmed by a fetched source.
- The crayfish suits an invasive pest at Chadwick Lakes.

### Gaps
- Not found:
  - freshwater shrimps (e.g. *Echinogammarus* / *Atyaephyra*);
  - endemic freshwater invertebrates of Chadwick Lakes, Baħrija or Wied il-Lunzjata;
  - the crab's size and colour.
