# Marine life of Maltese waters (Malta, Gozo, Comino, Filfla)

Research notes for a Minecraft content mod (Maltese sea creatures and food). Researched 2026-11-14 with web search and fetch.
Several primary sources could not be read: era.org.mt pages and its PDFs returned HTTP 403, agrikoltura.gov.mt (the government "List of Species" fish ID page) returned 403, and one UM news page returned 500. So Maltese names come mainly from the **EU commercial-designations database for Malta** (200 Maltese names with scientific names, read in full), University of Malta papers, and press reports of ERA and UM statements.
Spelling: names are given exactly as each source writes them. Several sources drop the Maltese diacritics (for example "Haddiela" for "Ħaddiela" or "Griza" for "Griża"). Where I could not confirm a form, it is flagged.

## 1. Marine mammals (dolphins, whales, monk seal)

### Takeaway
ERA's marine assessment lists 4 whale and 4 dolphin species in Maltese waters. Common, striped and bottlenose dolphins are seen all year. The Mediterranean monk seal (Maltese *bumarin*) was long counted as gone, but it has been sighted again since 2024. Gozo place names (Ta' Bumbarin, Għar il-Bumarini) remember it.

### Cited Findings
- ERA's *Assessment of Marine Waters* lists these species. Whales: fin whale (*Balaenoptera physalus*), sperm whale (*Physeter macrocephalus*), Cuvier's beaked whale (*Ziphius cavirostris*) and long-finned pilot whale (*Globicephala melas*). Dolphins: Risso's (*Grampus griseus*), common (*Delphinus delphis*), striped (*Stenella coeruleoalba*) and bottlenose (*Tursiops truncatus*). — [ERA press release](https://era.org.mt/press-releases/strong-presence-of-cetaceans-confirmed-around-the-maltese-islands/); [Malta Independent](https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2021-10-24/local-news/Strong-Presence-of-whales-and-dolphins-confirmed-around-the-Maltese-Islands-6736237782)
- ERA says common, striped and bottlenose dolphins are "the most frequently recorded all year round" and are commonly met by boaters. It says the other species are seen less often because they migrate and prefer deeper water. — [ERA press release](https://era.org.mt/press-releases/strong-presence-of-cetaceans-confirmed-around-the-maltese-islands/)
- Bottlenose dolphin: 59 pod sightings in 2013–2016, more often off the southern and western coasts. The 2014 population estimate was 79–224, with 79 judged the more realistic figure. The same surveys recorded 35 common dolphin sightings (mostly south-west of Malta) and 22 striped dolphin sightings. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/101632/maltas_bottlenose_dolphin_population_estimated_at_79)
- A habitat-suitability study (SAMS / UHI) treats Malta's coastal waters as a "human-altered environment" for bottlenose dolphins. — [SAMS](https://sams.ac.uk/science/research-papers/name-1224324-en.html)
- Risso's dolphin: a pair was sighted about 20 km off Malta's west coast. — [Lovin Malta](https://lovinmalta.com/malta/pair-of-rissos-dolphin-spotted-off-the-maltese-coast)
- A 2026 open letter reports that bottlenose dolphins in Maltese waters are harassed by boats, including feeding and chasing. — [Marine Connection open letter](https://marineconnection.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Open-Letter-Regarding-The-Ongoing-Harassment-Of-Wildlife-At-Sea.pdf)
- Mediterranean monk seal (*Monachus monachus*):
  - ERA reported a sighting in 2024, confirmed by Prof. Alan Deidun. — [UM Newspoint 2024](https://um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/2024/07/rare-monk-seal-spotted-in-maltese-waters); [MaltaToday](https://maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/130102/rare_mediterranean_monk_seal_sighting_reported_in_maltese_waters)
  - Malta Daily calls the earlier last Maltese record 1974. — [Malta Daily](https://maltadaily.mt/rare-sight-of-monk-seal-off-syracuse-last-in-malta-1974). Other reports say the late 1990s, so the sources conflict.
  - A page dated May 2026 reports two sightings within a few days, one of them in a sea cavern. — [divinginfo.mt](https://www.divinginfo.mt/monk-seal-sighting/)
  - A 2025 short communication in *Studia Marina* compiles the Maltese historical and recent records. — [Studia Marina](https://studiamarina.ucg.ac.me/assets/pdf/volume-198-42.pdf)
- The Gozo place name Ta' Bumbarin probably comes from *bumarin*, the Maltese word for the monk seal. A nearby sea cave, Għar il-Bumarini, hints that seals once lived there. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/where-the-monk-seal-once-swam-have-you-ever-explored-gozo-s-ta-bumbarin)
- The monk seal has been legally protected in Malta since 1992. It is critically endangered, with fewer than ~1,000 animals left, mainly in Greece, Cyprus and Turkey. — [Guide Me Malta, seal safety](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/seal-safety-here-are-some-ways-to-help-the-mediterranean-monk-seal-feel-at-home-in-malta)

### Inferences
- Game spawning:
  - common, striped and bottlenose dolphins: common, all year, near the coast (bottlenose especially south and west);
  - Risso's dolphin and the whales: rare, deep water offshore only;
  - monk seal: extremely rare, only in sea caves on the Gozo north and west coast. Good candidates are Ta' Bumbarin and the Dwejra and Blue Grotto caves, which the project already models.
- The 1:1 world's sea reaches ~10–15 km offshore. Sperm and fin whales belong to the deep Malta Trough / Malta Graben well beyond that, so they should be rare at the world's outer edge.
- Maltese dolphin names: *delfin* is the usual Maltese word, but I found no source for species-level Maltese names (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No source found for Maltese names of each dolphin or whale species. *Delfin* (dolphin) and *baleni* (whale) are believed common, but are not confirmed here.
- The fishermen's name *denfil* is unverified.
- No current, systematic sighting counts for the whales.
- No source tied Il-Ħofra to monk seals.

## 2. Sea turtles

### Takeaway
The loggerhead (*Caretta caretta*) is the regular species. It nests on the sandy beaches of Malta and Gozo: Għajn Tuffieħa, Golden Bay (Ir-Ramla tal-Mixquqa) and Ramla l-Ħamra. Green turtles are rare vagrants. Leatherbacks are occasional.

### Cited Findings
- Loggerhead nests have been found at Ramla l-Ħamra (Gozo) and Golden Bay (Ir-Ramla tal-Mixquqa; "fourth turtle nesting site at Golden Bay"). In one Gozo nest, 32 hatchlings hatched. — [MaltaToday, Ramla](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/environment/130218/loggerhead_turtle_nesting_site_discovered_at_ramla_lamra_); [MaltaToday, Golden Bay](https://maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/130275/fourth_turtle_nesting_site_at_golden_bay); [MaltaToday, Gozo hatch](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/124467/watch_32_loggerhead_turtles_hatch_in_gozo_)
- In 2024, Nature Trust Malta volunteers found a nest at Għajn Tuffieħa on a morning patrol. It was laid too close to the shore, so ERA and NTM moved the eggs to Ir-Ramla tal-Mixquqa and issued an Emergency Conservation Order. NTM's microchip check showed that one female laid more than one nest in a season. — [Lovin Malta](https://lovinmalta.com/lifestyle/pets/loggerhead-turtle-nest-discovered-in-ghajn-tuffieha); [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/spotted-another-nest-of-loggerhead-turtles-found-in-maltese-sands-at-g-ajn-tuffie-a)
- A 2024 UM conservation-genetics study looked at loggerhead reproductive behaviour in Maltese waters. — [UM OAR](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/122518/1/Conservation%20genetics%20of%20the%20Loggerhead%20sea%20turtle%20Caretta%20caretta%20from%20the%20central%20Mediterranean%20an%20insight%20into%20the%20species%20reproductive%20behaviour%20in%20Maltese%20waters%202024.pdf)
- Green turtle (*Chelonia mydas*), Maltese name *Fekruna hadra* (written without diacritics; correct form *Fekruna ħadra*). It is a vagrant: one was caught about a mile from Grand Harbour in 1929 and is kept in the Museum of Natural History. — [Sea turtles of the central Mediterranean](https://shadowservices.com/nature/Maltese/biology/mar-rep.htm); [UM "Sea turtles"](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/20427/1/Sea%20turtles.pdf)
- Leatherback (*Dermochelys coriacea*), Maltese *Fekruna Sewda*. Twelve were caught or sighted in 1970–1980. A dead juvenile stranded at Ċirkewwa on 16 July 2015, the first stranding record for Malta. — [shadowservices (Maltese biology)](https://shadowservices.com/nature/Maltese/biology/mar-rep.htm); [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/200847027.pdf)
- Sources conflict on how often leatherbacks occur. One popular guide says leatherbacks are recorded more often than the other species. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/at-loggerheads-watch-out-for-these-sea-turtles-in-maltese-waters). Others call them uncommon.

### Inferences
- *Fekruna* is the Maltese word for turtle. The loggerhead is probably *fekruna komuni*, but this is not confirmed (see Gaps).
- Game design: a loggerhead that lays eggs at night in summer on the sandy beaches above. Vanilla turtles already nest, so the mod could reskin or replace them. The beaches are Għajn Tuffieħa, Golden Bay, Ramla l-Ħamra and possibly Comino.

### Gaps
- The Maltese name of the loggerhead was not confirmed.
- Nesting months were not fetched. Mediterranean loggerheads typically nest June–August and hatch August–October, but this is not sourced here.

### Inferences
- Good game mobs:
  - colourful reef fish (*ċawl*, *marzpan*, the wrasses, *burqax*);
  - large solitary hunters (*ċerna*, *lizz*, *aċċola*);
  - hiding mobs that bite (*murina* and *gringu* in rock holes);
  - hazards (weevers in sand, *skorfna* on rock);
  - shoals (*vopa*, *sardina*, *xilpa* grazing Posidonia).
- Recognisable foods are *lampuka*, *awrata*, *ċerna*, *pagru*, *denċi*, *sargu* and *tonn*.

### Gaps
- The government fish ID list (agrikoltura.gov.mt) could not be read (403), so the EU list was not cross-checked against it.

- The common Maltese names "wrata" (given in the task) and "Awrata" (in the EU list) appear to be the same fish. The EU list uses "Awrata".

## 4. Lampuki season, fishing methods and dishes

### Takeaway
Lampuki season runs from about 15 August (Santa Marija) to December. Fishers catch them under *kannizzati*: floating rafts of palm fronds anchored by limestone, set from the traditional *luzzu* boats. The main ports are Marsaxlokk and Mġarr (Gozo). Signature dishes are *torta tal-lampuki*, *aljotta* and *stuffat tal-qarnit*.

### Cited Findings
- The season opens around 15 August. About 130 licensed vessels leave together at midnight, mostly from Marsaxlokk and Mġarr (Gozo), to set their *kannizzati*. Fishing runs to the end of December. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/it-s-lampuki-season-take-a-look-at-this-local-tradition-associated-with-malta-s-small-scale-fishing-sector); [Wikipedia: Lampuki netting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampuki_netting)
- A *kannizzata* is a fish aggregating device: a polystyrene float anchored by a heavy limestone block, with palm fronds that draw the fish into shade. Once fish gather, the nets go in. The rafts are towed out by *luzzu* boats or larger modern boats. — [Wikipedia: Lampuki netting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lampuki_netting); [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/it-s-lampuki-season-take-a-look-at-this-local-tradition-associated-with-malta-s-small-scale-fishing-sector)
- Each licensee lays at least 35 FADs. — [FAO country profile, Malta](https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/DOCUMENT/fcp/en/FI_CP_MT.pdf)
- Other news:
  - there are disputes with Tunisian fishers ("lampuki war"), and AFM patrol boats accompany Maltese fishers. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/98543/malta_fishers_win_kannizzati_concession_in_bid_to_quell_lampuki_war_with_tunisians); [MaltaToday AFM](https://maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/104174/maltese_armed_force)
  - a pilot project would start the season earlier. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/139385/pilot_project_to_start_lampuki_season_earlier_gets_commission_goahead__)
  - catches are reported down by half. — [EPP Group](https://www.eppgroup.eu/what-we-do/with-eu-countries/malta/mep-peter-agius-urges-action-as-lampuki-catches-drop-by-half)
  - line-caught lampuki are rising as net fishing declines. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/malta-s-favourite-fish-line-caught-lampuki-on-the-rise-as-traditional-net-fishing-declines)
  - boats are blessed in Marsaxlokk at the start of the season. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/tradition-in-port-blessings-in-marsaxlokk-to-mark-start-of-lampuki-season)
- *Aljotta*: a garlic and tomato fish soup, often made with lampuki, with rice, parsley, bay and oregano. It was traditionally eaten in Lent. — [Nomad Paradise](https://nomadparadise.com/maltese-food); [Kayak Malta food guide](https://www.kayak.co.uk/magazine/malta-food-guide)
- *Stuffat tal-qarnit*: octopus stew served with Maltese bread. It is also mixed into spaghetti (*spaghetti biz-zalza tal-qarnit*). — [Kayak Malta food guide](https://www.kayak.co.uk/magazine/malta-food-guide)
- *Torta tal-lampuki*: a lampuki pie with spinach, cauliflower, olives, capers and tomato, made in season (September–December). — [Nomad Paradise](https://nomadparadise.com/maltese-food)
- *Klamari mimlija* (stuffed squid) is listed as a traditional dish. — [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/what-to-eat-and-drink-in-malta)

### Inferences
- Game mechanic: a placeable *kannizzata* (a palm-frond raft) that attracts lampuki from August to December (in-game seasons or random). A *luzzu* boat entity painted in its traditional colours. Food items: torta tal-lampuki, aljotta, stuffat tal-qarnit, klamari mimlija and fried lampuki.

### Gaps
- No source fetched for these dishes: *ricci* (sea-urchin roe, said to be eaten with pasta) and *qarnit bit-tewm* (octopus with garlic).
- No source for the luzzu's design (colours, the Eye of Osiris on the bow). It is well known, but not cited here.

## 5. Sharks and rays

### Takeaway
Sharklab Malta knows of about 35 shark species historically in Maltese waters, 13 of them fully protected. Catsharks, nursehounds and angel sharks are the ones people meet. Blue sharks (*Ħuta kaħla*) are caught offshore and sold as frozen steaks.

### Cited Findings
- About 35 shark species have historically passed through Maltese waters, 13 with full protection (Sharklab's Greg Nowell). Another article says 37. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/shark-friendly-island-malta-leads-in-protection-of-the-mediterranean-s-finned-friends); [Lovin Malta](https://lovinmalta.com/news/local/maltese-man-boasts-about-capturing-and-killing-massive-shark)
- A rare dead angelshark (*Squatina squatina*) washed up at Marsaxlokk. Sharklab's market research led to the protection of angel sharks in Malta. — [Malta Daily](https://maltadaily.mt/dead-rare-angelshark-washes-up-in-marsaxlokk); [Sharklab history](https://www.tiki-toki.com/timeline/entry/1222230/sharklab-history/)

## 6. Seahorses and other protected fish

### Takeaway
Both Mediterranean seahorses live in Malta: *Hippocampus hippocampus* and *H. guttulatus*. Their Maltese name is *żiemel* or *żiemel tal-baħar*, and they have been fully protected since 2006.

### Cited Findings
- Both species occur in Malta. *H. guttulatus* (long-snouted / spiny seahorse) has the longer snout. Both have been fully protected under Maltese law since 2006. — [Malta SAC](https://maltasac.org/sea-horse); [Seahorse Trust Malta project](https://www.theseahorsetrust.org/conservation/malta-research-project/)
- Maltese names:
  - *Żiemel tal-Baħar* (National Literacy Agency). — [NLA](https://nla.gov.mt/?p=30403)
  - "Ziemel tal-bahar halqu qasir" (short-snouted) and "...halqu twil" (long-snouted), given without diacritics by FishBase via EUNIS. — [EUNIS H. hippocampus](https://eunis.eea.europa.eu/species/Hippocampus%20hippocampus); [EUNIS H. guttulatus](https://eunis.eea.europa.eu/species/Hippocampus%20guttulatus)
- A 2025 Bournemouth thesis on seahorses in Malta says both species are "considered to be endangered" and globally data deficient. The Seahorse Trust recorded the first repeat sighting of an individual seahorse in Malta. — [Bournemouth eprints](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/41588/); [Seahorse Trust](https://www.theseahorsetrust.org/?p=1621)

### Inferences
- The correct forms with diacritics are probably *żiemel tal-baħar ħalqu qasir* and *żiemel tal-baħar ħalqu twil*.
- Game: a rare, passive mob in seagrass meadows (Posidonia, Cymodocea) that cannot be caught or eaten, reflecting its legal protection.

## 7. Invasive / Lessepsian and other alien species

### Takeaway
Malta has a fast-growing list of alien species. The 2015 review names these as invasive: the alga *Caulerpa cylindracea*, the red algae *Lophocladia lallemandii* and *Womersleyella setacea*, the mussel *Brachidontes pharaonis*, the crab *Percnon gibbesi*, the bluespotted cornetfish *Fistularia commersonii* and the dusky spinefoot rabbitfish *Siganus luridus*. Lionfish (*Pterois miles*, first recorded 2016) and the poisonous silver-cheeked toadfish (*Lagocephalus sceleratus*, first recorded 2014) are spreading.

### Cited Findings
- Evans, Barbara & Schembri (2015), a critical review of alien marine species records from Malta, name these 8 as invasive: *Caulerpa cylindracea*, *Lophocladia lallemandi*, *Womersleyella setacea*, *Brachidontes pharaonis*, *Percnon gibbesi*, *Fistularia commersonii* and *Siganus luridus* (aliens), plus *Sphoeroides pachygaster* (a range extender). — [Heriot-Watt research portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/a-critical-review-of-records-of-alien-marine-species-from-the-mal/)
- An earlier review (Sciberras & Schembri 2007) shows alien records rising, with a peak of 22 species in 2001–2010. — [Med. Mar. Sci. (via EKT)](https://ejournals.epublishing.ekt.gr/index.php/hcmr-med-mar-sc/article/download/12904/12479). This is attributed from the search snippet; the full text was not read.
- Silver-cheeked toadfish (*Lagocephalus sceleratus*): first Maltese record in August 2014. It has spread west since its first Mediterranean record in Turkey in 2004. — [Deidun et al. 2015](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/26199/1/83Deidunetal2015Lagocephalussceleratus.pdf)
- Lionfish (*Pterois miles*):
  - first Maltese record in 2016. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/spotted-invasive-lionfish-confirmed-in-maltese-waters-for-the-first-time)
  - a reward was offered in 2019 for catching one. — [divinginfo.mt](https://www.divinginfo.mt/reward-spot-the-lionfish/)
  - a later surge in the Mediterranean raises concern for Malta. — [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/yikes-lionfish-surge-in-the-mediterranean-raises-alarm-for-malta-s-marine-life)
- Rabbitfish (dusky spinefoot, *Siganus luridus*):
  - present for about 15 years; numbers grew fast in 2024 once the water passed 30 °C;
  - it comes from the Red Sea;
  - UM and Ambjent Malta are making a cookbook for eating invasive species; the spines must be removed before cooking.

  — [UM Newspoint 2024](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/2024/07/rabbitfish-maltese-waters) (fetch failed; facts from the search summary)
- The Spot the Alien Fish citizen-science campaign is coordinated by Prof. Alan Deidun with a Malta–Tunisia–Sicily network. The first verified sighting of a target species wins a camera prize. Its launch date is given as both 2016 and 2017. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/71745/university_launches_spot_the_alien_fish_campaign_); [UM Spot the Alien](https://www.um.edu.mt/research/oceanographymalta/projects/spot-the-alien/). The sister site [aliensmalta.eu](https://www.aliensmalta.eu/) was not reviewed.
- A 2021 *Frontiers* study found native Mediterranean species preying on lionfish and the silver-cheeked toadfish. — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2021-07-food-chains-invasive-marine-species.pdf)
- Some alien fish in Malta are released aquarium fish. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/environment/67903/imported_acquarium_fish_wreaking_havoc_ecosystems)
- Blue Lagoon (Comino) has an alien *Caulerpa* and the alien seagrass *Halophila stipulacea* next to native Posidonia and Cymodocea. — [Oceana 2016](https://europe.oceana.org/blog/encounter-alien-species/)

### Inferences
- Game:
  - lionfish: a venomous-spine mob that is good to eat once its spines are removed, rewarding culling;
  - silver-cheeked toadfish: poisonous like a pufferfish (vanilla pufferfish mechanics fit);
  - rabbitfish: shoals that graze algae, with venomous spines;
  - *Percnon gibbesi* (nimble spray crab): a flat crab on the surge-zone rocks;
  - *Caulerpa cylindracea*: a bright green, spreading "carpet" block.

### Gaps
- No Maltese names found for the alien species. They are often called by their English or Italian names.
- *Stephanolepis diaspros* (reticulated leatherjacket) has no Maltese record in the results.
- The first-record years of *Fistularia* and *Siganus* in Malta were not found.

## 12. Cnidarians (jellyfish, corals, anemones) and sponges

### Takeaway
The mauve stinger (*Pelagia noctiluca*) is the commonest jellyfish and blooms in swarms (e.g. Anchor Bay). Over 40 gelatinous species are known from Malta. The deep sea around Malta holds living Lophelia and Madrepora coral reefs and the world's deepest known red coral (1,016 m).

### Cited Findings
- *Pelagia noctiluca* is among the most common jellyfish in Malta and the Mediterranean. Maltese has no specific word for it. — [UM jellyfish campaign 10 years](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/features/2020/01/jellyfish-campaign-10)
- Swarms invaded Anchor Bay. — [Lovin Malta](https://lovinmalta.com/lifestyle/watch-swarms-of-mauve-stinger-jellyfish-invade-anchor-bay-in-haunting-underwater-footage/)
- Fried-egg jellyfish (*Cotylorhiza tuberculata*) is among the five most reported species and blooms in August–November. — [UM jellyfish 10 years](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/features/2020/01/jellyfish-campaign-10)
- Other species reported:
  - Portuguese man o' war (*Physalia physalis*): the main health concern, with bulletins issued;
  - barrel jellyfish (*Rhizostoma pulmo*);
  - by-the-wind sailor (*Velella velella*);
  - multi-ribbed jellyfish (*Aequorea forskalea*);
  - uncommon "blue button" (*Porpita porpita*) and "cigar" species.

  Over 40 gelatinous species are known, nine of them new to Malta since the campaign began in June 2010. — [UM jellyfish 10 years](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/features/2020/01/jellyfish-campaign-10); [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/3250/jellyfish-spotting-campaign-reports-uncommon-blue-button-and-cigar-species); [Ten years of jellyfish spotting (PDF)](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/66904/1/Ten_years_of_jellyfish_spotting_2020.pdf)
- Thousands of jellyfish have beached along Malta's coast. — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/99957/thousands_of_jellyfish_beached_along_maltas_coastline)
- Maltese names:
  - common jellyfish: "Brama Komuni";
  - sea anemone: "Artikla" (on rocky shores in very shallow water);
  - bristle worm (fireworm): "Busuf" (on rocky bottoms; its venomous hairs itch for hours).

  — [MaltaToday](https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/environment/nature/111247/critters_by_the_seashore_watch_out_for_these_dangerous_sea_creatures)
- Deep corals:
  - living *Lophelia pertusa* and *Madrepora oculata* at 390–617 m. — [Schembri et al. 2007](https://openpolar.no/Record/ftunivmalta:oai:www.um.edu.mt:123456789%2F5507/Description)
  - LIFE BaĦAR ROV surveys (2015, Oceana Ranger, inside the 25-nm zone, Malta Trough) found these corals deeper than 500 m, with areas dominated by the gorgonian *Callogorgia verticillata*. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/83022179.pdf)
  - red coral (*Corallium rubrum*) grows down to 1,016 m, a world depth record. — [UM Newspoint 2016](https://www.um.edu.mt/newspoint/news/2016/10/newworldrecordmaltahasthedeepestknownpopulationsofpreciousredcoral)
  - other habitat formers are the black coral *Leiopathes glaberrima* and *Callogorgia*. — [UM dissertation 2020](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/81307)
  - five offshore MPAs were proposed to protect reefs and sea caves. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/83022179.pdf)

### Inferences
- *Brama* appears to be the generic Maltese word for jellyfish.
- Game:
  - mauve stinger: a glowing night swarm (*noctiluca* means "night light");
  - Portuguese man o' war: a floating surface hazard with strong poison, blown ashore by wind;
  - fried-egg jellyfish: harmless and large in late summer;
  - anemones: stinging blocks;
  - fireworm: a contact-damage mob.
- Deep coral reef blocks (white Lophelia/Madrepora, red coral) belong in the deep trough well beyond the shelf. Red coral in shallow sea caves (the cave sites the project models) is plausible but not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No sourced information on Malta's shallow-water corals (e.g. *Cladocora caespitosa*, *Astroides calycularis*) or on sea-cave fauna.
- No information on sponges, beyond the deep suspension-feeder assemblages.
- No Maltese names for corals or sponges. *Qroll* (coral) and *sponża* (sponge) are believed common, but are unverified.

## 13. Seagrasses and algae

### Takeaway
Posidonia oceanica (Neptune grass) meadows are the main shallow habitat on sand and rock. Cymodocea nodosa meadows are extensive. The alien seagrass *Halophila stipulacea* and the invasive green alga *Caulerpa cylindracea* are present, including at Blue Lagoon (Comino).

### Cited Findings
- A UM baseline survey (1991–97) documents Posidonia meadows among the main benthic communities. A Cymodocea bed forms a major association in the fine-sand community, and *Halophila* meadows occur in limited areas. — [CORE baseline](https://core.ac.uk/download/46603497.pdf)
- The Cymodocea meadows recorded in a UM MPA pilot study are among the most extensive known from the Maltese Islands. — [UM MPA pilot study](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/5264/1/pilot%20study%20aimed%20at%20the%20establishment%20of%20Marine%20Protected%20Areas.pdf)
- ERA describes Posidonia beds (Neptune grass) as a protected habitat, sensitive to anchoring, overfishing and pollution. — [ERA marine habitats](https://era.org.mt/topic/marine-habitats-2/)
- In 2016, Blue Lagoon (Comino) still had Posidonia and Cymodocea, plus an alien *Caulerpa* and *Halophila stipulacea*. — [Oceana](https://europe.oceana.org/blog/encounter-alien-species/)
- *Caulerpa cylindracea*, *Lophocladia lallemandii* and *Womersleyella setacea* (algae) are invasive in Malta. — [Heriot-Watt portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/a-critical-review-of-records-of-alien-marine-species-from-the-mal/)

### Inferences
- Game:
  - Posidonia: tall seagrass blocks in dense meadows from shallow water to about 35–40 m (Mediterranean norm; depth not sourced for Malta). Dead leaves wash up as banks ("banquettes") on beaches;
  - Cymodocea: shorter, paler grass on sand;
  - Halophila: tiny oval leaves;
  - Caulerpa: a spreading bright-green carpet that can replace native blocks.
- Algae that colour the rocks:
  - pink coralline crusts (*Lithophyllum*);
  - brown *Cystoseira* forests;
  - *Padina* fans;
  - green *Ulva* at the waterline.

  These are Mediterranean-typical, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No Maltese names for seagrasses or algae were sourced. *Alka* for Posidonia is unverified.
- No Maltese data found for Posidonia depth limits or meadow cover.
- No source found for the rock-colouring algae of Malta's coasts.
