# Flora, crops and fungi of the Maltese Islands (for the MiM content mod)

Scope: wild plants of Malta, Gozo, Comino, Filfla and the islets; traditional crops and cultivated plants; fungi, the "Maltese fungus" (a parasitic plant), and brief notes on lichens and mosses.

How to read the source marks:
- Items with an inline [Source](URL) were confirmed during this research (searches and pages read 2026-11-14).
- Items marked **[BK]** come from background botanical knowledge and were NOT confirmed against a source in this pass. They are given because the brief asks for an exhaustive-as-practical list, but each one (especially Maltese spellings, seasons and localities) must be checked against maltawildplants.com (Stephen Mifsud) or Weber & Kendzior / Haslam, Sell & Wolseley before it goes into the mod.
- Maltese names from Nature Trust Malta's habitat page are cited to it. Some of its spellings differ slightly from Mifsud's (noted where seen).

## 1. National plant and national tree

### Takeaway
The national plant is the endemic Maltese rock-centaury, *widnet il-baħar* (*Cheirolophus crassifolius*, syn. *Palaeocyanus crassifolius*), a cliff shrub. The national tree is the sandarac gum tree, *għargħar* (*Tetraclinis articulata*), a native conifer that is now rare. Both are strictly protected.

### Cited Findings
- **Maltese rock-centaury / widnet il-baħar**: *Palaeocyanus crassifolius* (also written *Cheirolophus crassifolius*). It is the national plant under the Species Protection (Designation of National Species) Regulations, S.L. 549.120, in force since 24 September 2024, [ERA national species (via search)](https://era.org.mt/topic/national-species/) (the ERA page itself returned 403 to the fetch tool; the facts come from the search summary).
- It is an endemic **monotypic** genus and a cliff (rupestral) plant, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/). A Gozo flora study names *Palaeocyanus* (and *Cremnophyton*) as the islands' two monospecific endemic genera, [The flora and vegetation of Gozo 1997](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/76563/1/The_flora_and_vegetation_of_Gozo_1997.pdf).
- Distribution: a perennial rupicolous shrub on the north-western and southern limestone cliffs of Malta and Gozo, [IUCN Med Plant Specialist Group Top 50](https://iucn-mpsg.org/species/22). Other sources say it grows on the southern cliffs of Malta, southern Gozo and Fungus Rock; that it is "quite common in the limits of Wied Babu"; and that it is "scarce but widespread" on Malta's western cliffs and rare on Gozo's southern cliffs (the sources disagree), [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/discover-the-local-rock-centaury-malta-s-endangered-and-strictly-protected-floral-gem); [World Plants](https://worldplants.ca/display.php?id=6666). Natura 2000 site listing: L-Inħawi ta' Ta' Ċenċ (Gozo), [EUNIS](https://eunis.eea.europa.eu/species/319399).
- **Sandarac gum tree / għargħar** (*Tetraclinis articulata*, cypress family). Maltese names Siġra tal-Għargħar, Għargħar. Indigenous, present before humans arrived. Once believed common, now "restricted in few sites and quite rare", mostly on escarpment edges; also planted as an ornamental in parks. A pyramidal, monoecious tree 12–15 m tall with flattened twigs, leaves in fours and cones 8–12 mm. Flowers all year but inconspicuously. Strictly protected: Schedule I of S.L. 549.123 (Trees and Woodlands Protection Regulations); Schedules III and VI of S.L. 549.44; listed as threatened in the 1989 Red Data Book, [maltawildplants (Mifsud)](https://maltawildplants.com/CUPR/Tetraclinis_articulata.php).
- It was declared National Tree in 1992. The name għargħar comes from Arabic ʿarʿar, [Wikipedia: Tetraclinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetraclinis). The Phoenician juniper (*Juniperus phoenicea*) has the same Maltese name, which causes confusion, [BirdLife Malta](https://birdlifemalta.org/2016/03/a-tree-between-two-continents/).

### Inferences
- Game design: widnet il-baħar suits a rare cliff-face "flower" block (a woody shrub with purple-pink centaury heads, [BK] flowering in spring–early summer) that generates only on high coastal cliffs (Dingli, Ta' Ċenċ, Wied Babu). The għargħar suits a rare conifer sapling or tree, cypress-like, on escarpment edges.

### Gaps
- Exact flowering months and the number of wild għargħar stands. [BK]: the main wild stands are thought to be in the Wied il-Bużbież / Mġarr / Mellieħa area of north-west Malta; this was not confirmed here.
- The full ERA national-species list (it may include a national bird and others) could not be read (HTTP 403).

- About 25 endemics is a manageable set for a "Maltese endemic" collection (a herbarium book or an advancement). Nearly all are cliff or garrigue plants, so they fit as rare surface decorations on cliff tops (Dingli, Ta' Ċenċ, Xlendi, Sanap) and on karst garrigue.

## 3. Native trees, woodland and large shrubs

### Takeaway
Native woodland survives only as small remnants ("living fossils"), mainly holm oak with Aleppo pine, olive and carob, plus maquis of lentisk, bay laurel and sandarac. Buskett is semi-natural woodland, largely planted. Ballut tal-Wardija holds the oldest holm oaks (500–900 years). The Bidnija olives (Bidni cultivar) are among the oldest trees in Malta.

### Cited Findings
- Woodland: Evergreen (holm) oak **Balluta** (*Quercus ilex*), Aleppo pine **Żnuber** (*Pinus halepensis*), olive **Żebbuġ** (*Olea europaea*), carob **Ħarrub** (*Ceratonia siliqua*). Maquis: lentisk **Deru** (*Pistacia lentiscus*), bay laurel **Rand** (*Laurus nobilis*), carob, olive and sandarac gum tree **Għargħar**. Woodland was "almost totally exterminated"; the remnants are "living fossils of ancient forests". **Buskett**: semi-natural woodland, largely planted, where trees regenerate naturally. **Ballut tal-Wardija**: the largest woodland remnant, with trees thought to be 500–900 years old. Afforestation woods (none natural): Miżieb, L-Aħrax tal-Mellieħa, Pietà, Fort Chambray (Mġarr, Gozo), Kennedy Grove (Salini), Wied Għollieqa, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- Tamarisk: African tamarisk **Siġra tal-Bruk** (*Tamarix africana*), coastal and dunes, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- Cliff shrubs: wolfbane **Siġra tal-Ħarir** (*Periploca angustifolia*); Egyptian St John's wort **Fexfiex ta' l-Irdum** (*Triadenia aegyptica*), [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- **Bidni olive**: an indigenous cultivar with its own DNA profile; some trees are declared national monuments. The Bidnija grove kept only 20 of the 40 trees it had in the early 20th century and was declared a tree-protected site. Heavy pruning, felling or uprooting of olive trees, Bidni included, needs a permit. Its age is disputed: some argue for the 1st century AD, others for the mid-to-late medieval period, [Wikipedia: Bidni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidni). Press report: "2000 year old trees still producing olives", [Malta Independent 2011](https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2011-08-16/news/2000-year-old-trees-still-producing-olives-297296/). A UM paper calls the grove probably the oldest in the country, possibly Roman, [UM, The white olives of Malta](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22699/1/the%20white%20olives%20of%20malta.pdf). Michelin says the name refers to the valley around Bidnija; Wikipedia disputes the link to the hamlet, [Michelin](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/travel/bidni-olive-oil-a-one-of-a-kind-product-from-malta).
- [BK, unconfirmed] Other native or long-established trees and large shrubs, for verification:
  - Sicilian sumac, *Rhus coriaria* (xumakk);
  - chaste tree, *Vitex agnus-castus* (għadiba);
  - white poplar, *Populus alba* (luq);
  - narrow-leaved ash, *Fraxinus angustifolia* (fraxxnu);
  - field elm, *Ulmus minor* (ulmu);
  - Maltese/Mediterranean willow, *Salix pedicellata* (żafżafa), in valley beds;
  - wild fig, *Ficus carica* (tin; the wild caprifig);
  - Mediterranean hawthorn / azarole, *Crataegus azarolus*, and *C. monogyna* (żagħrun);
  - Mediterranean buckthorn, *Rhamnus alaternus* and *R. lycioides* subsp. *oleoides*;
  - Phoenician juniper, *Juniperus phoenicea* (also called għargħar);
  - myrtle, *Myrtus communis* (riħan);
  - wild olive, *Olea europaea* var. *sylvestris* (żebbuġ salvaġġ);
  - Judas tree, *Cercis siliquastrum*;
  - cork oak, *Quercus suber* (a few old trees, possibly planted);
  - *Quercus virgiliana* (rare deciduous oaks, e.g. Buskett and Wardija);
  - Hawthorn-leaved / Sicilian maple (none confirmed).

### Inferences
- A tree set for the mod: balluta (holm oak), żnuber (Aleppo pine), ħarrub (carob, with pods), żebbuġ (olive, with an "ancient Bidni olive" variant: a gnarled trunk), għargħar (sandarac), rand (bay), bruka (tamarisk). These cover most native woodland and maquis looks.

### Gaps
- Exact locations and sizes of the Wied il-Bużbież woodland and other remnants were not confirmed here.
- Maltese names marked [BK] above need checking.

## 4. Garrigue, maquis, steppe and common wildflowers

### Takeaway
The open karst country is garrigue (thyme, heath, rosemary, spurge, orchids) and steppe (esparto grass, asphodel, squill). Disturbed fields and roadsides carry the familiar spring colour: crown daisy, Bermuda buttercup, poppy, borage and milk thistle.

### Cited Findings
- **Garrigue**: Mediterranean thyme **Sagħtar** (*Thymbra capitata*); Mediterranean heath **Erika** (*Erica multiflora*); rosemary **Klin** (*Rosmarinus officinalis*); Maltese spurge **Tengħud tax-Xagħri** (*Euphorbia melitensis*); Maltese pyramidal orchid, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- **Steppe**: esparto grass **Ħalfa** (*Lygeum spartum*); branched asphodel **Berwieq** (*Asphodelus aestivus*); seaside squill **Għansar** (*Urginea maritima*), [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).

## 5. Coastal, cliff, dune, saline marsh, wetland and valley plants

### Takeaway
The cliffs are the main refuge for endemics. Sand dunes (Ramla l-Ħamra, Għadira, Golden Bay and others [BK]) are tiny and threatened, and marram grass is already extinct on them. The saltmarshes (Salina, Marsaxlokk/Il-Għadira ta' San Rafflu, Is-Simar, Għadira [BK for some]) hold rushes, sea-lavenders and glassworts. Freshwater valley plants are rare because the habitat is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- **Cliffs**: Maltese rock-centaury, cliff-orache, salt-tree, sea-lavender, fleabane, everlasting; Egyptian St John's wort; rock crosswort **Kruċanella** (*Crucianella rupestris*); wolfbane **Siġra tal-Ħarir** (*Periploca angustifolia*); Sicilian snapdragon **Papoċċa Bajda** (*Antirrhinum siculum*). The cliffs are "the last refuge from human interference", [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- **Coastal halophytes**: Zerafa's sea-lavender; Maltese sea camomile; African tamarisk; shrubby glasswort **Almeridja tal-Blat** (*Arthrocnemum macrostachyum*); golden samphire **Xorbett** (*Inula crithmoides*, = *Limbarda crithmoides*; FoE spells it Xorbebb/Xorbett); smooth-leaved saltwort **Ħaxixa ta' l-Irmied** (*Salsola soda*), [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- Rock samphire / sea fennel **Busbies il-baħar** (*Crithmum maritimum*): flowers June–September, on coastal rocks, cliffs and walls, largely absent inland; pickled and eaten in salads, [FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/foraging-guide).
- **Saline marshland (Marsaxlokk, nature reserve 1993)**: seaside sea-lavender **Limonju tal-Baħar** (*Limonium virgatum*); sharp rush **Simar Niggież** (*Juncus acutus*); hollow-leaved rush **Simar ta' l-Ilma** (*Juncus subulatus*); sea rush **Simar tal-Baħar** (*Juncus maritimus*). "Simar" (rushes) gives the name of the Is-Simar wetland, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- **Sand dunes**: marram grass (*Ammophila littoralis*), **reported extinct**; sand couch (*Elytrigia juncea*); dropseed (*Sporobolus arenarius*); African tamarisk; sea rocket (*Cakile maritima*); sea holly (*Eryngium maritimum*); sea daffodil (*Pancratium maritimum*). Dunes are "threatened habitats which contain some very rare plants", [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- **Freshwater and valleys**: giant reed **Qasba Kbira** (*Arundo donax*); freshwater species are rare because the habitats are scarce, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/). Alexanders and bramble in valley beds, [FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/foraging-guide).
- [BK, unconfirmed]: common reed *Phragmites australis*; bulrush *Typha*; maidenhair fern *Adiantum capillus-veneris* on wet valley rocks (e.g. Wied il-Lunzjata, Gozo; Chadwick Lakes / Wied il-Qlejja); willow *Salix pedicellata* (żafżafa); white poplar; oleander *Nerium oleander* (planted); sea daffodil (narċis il-baħar), flowering August–September on sand; seagrass *Posidonia* (marine, often washed up as banquettes).

### Inferences
- These map onto the world's existing coast types (cliffs, sand beaches, saltpans, wied beds). The dune set is small (sea holly, sea rocket, sea daffodil, sand couch, tamarisk), which suits Ramla l-Ħamra and Għadira.

### Gaps
- Lists for the Is-Simar, Għadira and Salina reserves were not read. Plants of Filfla and the islets (e.g. the Filfla wall lizard's plant habitat; [BK] Filfla has few plant species) were not researched.

## 6. Introduced and invasive plants

### Takeaway
Nine EU "Union concern" invasive species are present in Malta, including *Acacia saligna* and *Ailanthus altissima*. Bermuda buttercup (*Oxalis pes-caprae*) carpets the countryside every spring; sources disagree on how damaging it is. Carpobrotus is being removed from Gozo cliffs.

### Cited Findings
- ERA: 9 IAS of Union concern are present in the Maltese Islands, *Acacia saligna* among them, [ERA](https://era.org.mt/topic/ias-an-overview/).
- *Ailanthus altissima*, tree-of-heaven, Maltese **ix-xumakk / ix-xumakk falz** ("false sumac"), Union-concern listed. A Malta eradication guide rates it very difficult to remove; *Acacia saligna* difficult (its seed bank stays viable for years); *Carpobrotus acinaciformis* very easy; *Oxalis pes-caprae* the hardest, [UICN Med plants presentation](https://medplantsweek.uicnmed.org/public_html/medplantsweek/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/AC05.pdf) (date not confirmed).
- *Oxalis pes-caprae* (Ħaxixa Ingliża, "English weed") "literally carpets whole areas", [ERA](https://era.org.mt/topic/ias-an-overview/). Nature Trust: "not a particularly invasive species" but it dominates the landscape, [Nature Trust Malta flora](https://www.naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/flora). On Comino the effectively invasive species are *Acacia saligna*, *Ailanthus altissima* and *Oxalis pes-caprae* (search summary of a Comino flora study).
- Kaffir fig (*Carpobrotus* spp.): considerable environmental and socio-economic impacts; removed from Gozo cliffs, [ERA](https://era.org.mt/topic/ias-an-overview/).
- Council of Europe report on invasive alien species in Malta, [CoE](https://rm.coe.int/invasive-alien-species-in-malta/16807303b0); Baldacchino paper, [UM OAR](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar//bitstream/123456789/23693/1/Alfred%20E.%20Baldacchino.pdf).
- Prickly pear (*Opuntia ficus-indica*, Bajtar tax-xewk / Ċuplajs): an introduced plant used as live fences around fields and old farmsteads; fruit in summer and early autumn, [FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/foraging-guide).
- Castor oil tree (Siġra tar-Riċnu), acacias and eucalyptus are aliens of disturbed ground, [Nature Trust Malta](https://naturetrustmalta.org/environmental-education/biodiversity/habitats/terrestrian/).
- [BK, unconfirmed]: false acacia *Robinia pseudoacacia*; eucalyptus (*Eucalyptus camaldulensis*, the ewkaliptu of the afforestation sites); *Agave americana*; fountain grass *Pennisetum setaceum*; *Nicotiana glauca* (tree tobacco); *Opuntia* history (brought from the Americas, probably 16th–17th century).

### Inferences
- The invasives give a believable gameplay hook (removal / clearing), and they shape the real look of the countryside (yellow Oxalis carpets in winter and spring, acacia thickets).

### Gaps
- The full list of the nine Union-concern species was not obtained (ERA pages returned limited content).

## 7. Crops and cultivated plants of traditional Maltese farming

### Takeaway
Maltese farming is dominated by forage (67.5% of the utilised agricultural area in 2020), early potatoes for export, vegetables (tomatoes for kunserva paste), and vines and olives. Capers, prickly pears, carobs and figs are classic rural plants. The indigenous grapes are Ġellewża (red) and Girgentina (white); the indigenous olive is the Bidni.

### Cited Findings
- Agricultural census 2020: 10,281 holdings (−16.2% since 2010, when there were 12,268). Forage is the main arable use: 5,252 ha, 67.5% of the UAA, [Malta Independent 2022](https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2022-12-02/local-news/Number-of-agricultural-holdings-decreased-by-16-2-since-2010-6736247917); [NSO Agricensus 2020](https://nso.gov.mt/themes_publications/agricensus-2020/).
- **Potatoes (patata)**: about 570 ha, 7.3% of arable land (2020; the figure also appears for 2007, so it needs checking against the census); 1,207 ha in 2003 → 712 ha in 2007; 689 ha in 2013. Early potatoes are exported to Europe in March–May, [Spudsmart](https://spudsmart.com/malta-potato-industry-decline/); [MaltaToday archive](https://archive.maltatoday.com.mt/2009/03/04/t1.html); [NSO methods](https://nso.gov.mt/themes_sources___met/crop-production/).
- **Tomatoes**: the NSO gets processed-tomato data from private processors, [NSO](https://nso.gov.mt/themes_sources___met/crop-production/). **Kunserva**: a very thick tomato paste spread on bread (ħobż biż-żejt); called "the red gold of Gozo" in a June 2026 feature linked to Gozo's European Region of Gastronomy bid, [El Español](https://www.elespanol.com/cocinillas/reportajes-gastronomicos/20260607/kunserva-oro-rojo-gozo-pasta-tomate-convertido-isla-region-europea-gastronomia/1003744268601_0.html); [Visit Gozo](https://visitgozo.com/2024/04/24/have-you-tried-all-these-preserves-and-delicacies-on-the-island-of-gozo/). Sun-dried tomatoes **tadam imqadded** are salted and dried in the summer sun, [Malta Products](https://www.maltaproducts.com/product-category/gourmet-craft-food/).
- **Sulla** (*Hedysarum coronarium* / *Sulla coronaria*; Maltese silla [BK]): a perennial, deep-rooted, drought-resistant legume 1–1.5 m tall with red flowers, grown for fodder, hay and honey; native range includes Malta, [Wikipedia: Sulla coronaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulla_coronaria). GRIN lists it as naturalised in Malta, [ILRI GRIN](https://genebank.ilri.org/gringlobal/taxon/taxonomydetail?id=18615) (sources conflict on native vs naturalised).
- **Capers**: the Maltese caper is given as *Capparis spinosa* ("Kappar tax-xewk") in popular sources but as mostly *C. orientalis* by FoE (*C. spinosa* is rarer). Buds are hand-picked and brined for at least 3 weeks. Gozo valleys are a traditional foraging place, but wild picking is now discouraged because of past overpicking. Flowers last one day, [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/did-you-know-that-the-flowers-from-which-malta-get-its-capers-only-live-for-one-day); [A Maltese Pantry](https://www.amaltesepantry.com/condiments/gozo-island-of-capers/); [FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/foraging-guide).
- **Grapes**: the indigenous **Ġellewża** (red, now often bottled alone, also rosé; anthocyanins 708 mg/kg) and **Girgentina** (white, crisp and fruity, usually blended with Chardonnay; highest acidity). Both are grown in small parcels averaging just over 0.1 ha. Over 30 varieties are in the DOK protocols. The National Viticulture & Oenology Centre at Buskett is recovering lost indigenous varieties, [UM OAR 19992](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/19992); [Horeca Malta 2026](https://horecamalta.com.mt/2026/08/09/the-novelty-of-old/); [Oh My Malta](https://ohmymalta.com.mt/2026/02/27/meet-maltas-indigenous-wine-grapes/).
- **Olives**: Bidni (see section 3). Malta also has rare "white olives", [UM, The white olives of Malta](https://um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/22699/1/the%20white%20olives%20of%20malta.pdf).
- **Prickly pear** (fruit, pads, live fences), **carob**, **fig** (FoE: under the shade of carob, fig, almond and prickly pear), [FoE Malta](https://foemalta.org/foraging-guide).

## 8. Fungi, the "Maltese fungus", lichens and mosses

### Takeaway
The famous "Maltese fungus" is not a fungus. It is *Cynomorium coccineum*, a red parasitic flowering plant on Fungus Rock (Il-Ġebla tal-Ġeneral, Dwejra, Gozo). The Knights guarded it and sent it to royalty as a styptic and as a cure for dysentery. Real macrofungi are poorly studied: about 50 species were first listed in 1986, and about 160 were recorded on mainland Malta by the early 2000s.

### Cited Findings
- **Fungus Rock / Il-Ġebla tal-Ġeneral ("The General's Rock")**: a limestone lump about 60 m high at the entrance of the circular Dwejra lagoon; area 0.007 km², coastline 494 m; San Lawrenz jurisdiction; now a nature reserve. The Knights Hospitaller found the "Maltese fungus" on its flat top, a parasitic flowering plant with a repulsive smell. They used it as a styptic wound dressing and a dysentery cure, and gave it as gifts to noblemen and visitors. In 1746 Grand Master Pinto declared the rock out of bounds, on pain of 3 years as an oarsman in the galleys. He posted a permanent guard, had a cable-car basket run 50 m to the mainland, and had the sides smoothed to remove handholds, [Wikipedia: Fungus Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungus_Rock).
- It was called "fungus melitensis" (Maltese mushroom) and sent to European royalty. Dwejra Tower (1651) was built partly to guard Fungus Rock, [Wikipedia: Cynomorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynomorium); [Mifsud article](https://maltawildplants.com/CYNM/Docs/CYMCC/Cynomorium_%20Parasitic%20Plant%20Widely%20Used%20in%20Traditional%20Medicine.html). The 1746 date and the punishment rest on one or two sources.
- The rock also hosts the national plant widnet il-baħar, [Guide Me Malta](https://www.guidememalta.com/en/discover-the-local-rock-centaury-malta-s-endangered-and-strictly-protected-floral-gem).
- **Macrofungi**: 50 species recorded for the first time in an annotated 1986 list (Lanfranco), [CMN 1(4) 1986, UM OAR](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/14542/1/CMN-1-4-1986-A1.pdf). Additions by Briffa (2002, *Central Mediterranean Naturalist*), [UM OAR 14936](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/14936). A Gozo-focused paper puts the mainland Malta total at about 160 species, [Borziana 4](https://www.herbmedit.org/Borziana/Borziana4_051-094.pdf) (attribution from the search summary; not read in full). A UM "MycoMalta" feature exists, [THINK 41](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/bitstream/123456789/132252/1/THINK41-MycoMalta.pdf).
- No source confirmed *Pleurotus eryngii* (or any named edible mushroom) as wild in Malta.

### Inferences
- For the mod: "Għerq Sinjur / Maltese fungus" [BK: a Maltese name is not confirmed; it is sometimes called "il-Għerq tal-Ġeneral"] as a dark-red club-shaped parasitic plant that spawns only on the Fungus Rock top. It could be a medicine item (stops bleeding) with a guard / "forbidden" lore hook.
- Real Maltese edible fungi are poorly documented. Keep fungi content minimal or generic (e.g. [BK] *Pleurotus eryngii* on *Eryngium* / *Ferula* roots and *Agaricus* field mushrooms are plausible for the Mediterranean but unconfirmed for Malta).

### Gaps
- No Maltese edible-mushroom list was found; the THINK MycoMalta and Borziana papers should be read.
- **Lichens and mosses**: no source was read in this pass. [BK] Malta has a few hundred lichen taxa (orange *Xanthoria* / *Caloplaca* on coastal rocks and rubble walls are conspicuous) and roughly 100+ bryophytes (Lanfranco and others), but no counts were confirmed.
- **Total vascular flora**: a search summary cites a 2013 University of Malta paper giving "approximately 1,100 indigenous, archaeophytic and naturalised alien species". The exact URL was not confirmed, so the figure is unverified (it matches the brief's ~1,100+).
