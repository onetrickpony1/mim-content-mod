# Food systems (draft 3)

**State:** design only. None of these systems is built; only food art is in the mod so far ([[Food progress]]).

**What shaped it:**
- [[2026-11-17 Food – go bigger, new art and systems, a materially Maltese change]]: food must change the game in a Maltese way, with new art and stations allowed.
- [[2026-11-17 Food – bonuses not penalties, food is one part of the mod, no other-mod links]]:
  - **food is one part of the mod, not all of it**;
  - **good Maltese practice earns a bonus**, and vanilla farming is never made harder;
  - no links to other mods;
  - Maltese dishes are made only on their own Maltese blocks;
  - freshness is off;
  - rennet comes from both lambs and plants.

[[Food design]] (draft 1) is **the catalogue**: crops, ingredients, the 68 dishes and their values. This note is **how food is made and what it adds to the game**.

Marks: **✓** = checked against a source (listed at the end); **†** = general knowledge, still to check.

---

## The idea

Vanilla farming stays exactly as it is: a wheat farm next to water still works. What the mod adds is **a Maltese way of making food**: the stone stove, the wood oven, the salt pans, the cheese baskets, the presses and the fish traps. Every one is a block you build and use, and the dishes it makes are **better than anything in vanilla**. Players who build a well-watered terrace, cook on a kenur and keep a cellar get more out of their food. Players who don't lose nothing.

## The six systems

| # | System | What it adds | Rule it follows | Art |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Il-Kċina**: the kitchen | The kenur stove, pots and pans, placed dishes, the table bonus | The only place Maltese dishes are cooked | high |
| 2 | **Il-Forn**: bread | The wood-fired oven, the sourdough starter, the hand quern; the windmill later | Bread, ftira, pastries and oven dishes are baked only here | high |
| 3 | **Il-Kantina**: the pantry | Salt pans, drying racks, jars, the cheese baskets and drying cage | Makes the preserved ingredients the dishes need | medium |
| 4 | **L-Għalqa**: the field (light) | An irrigation **bonus**, an in-season **bonus**, Maltese beehives | Only adds; vanilla growth is untouched | medium |
| 5 | **Il-Magħsra**: presses | The olive press, the grape press, barrels | Oil and wine for the dishes; a cool cellar makes better wine | high |
| 6 | **Il-Baħar**: the sea | Cane fish traps, lampuki rafts | Extra ways to fish; vanilla fishing unchanged | medium |

**Each system has its own on/off switch** in the mod's config. **Freshness is not part of the design** (owner: off).

## 1. Il-Kċina: the kitchen

**Real Malta:**
- The **kenur** was the Maltese stone (or clay) stove, carved from Maltese stone and burning wood or coal ✓.
- Cooks **fanned the flames** ✓, and it was in wide use until the early 20th century ✓.

**In the game:**
- **Il-kenur** (a 2-block-wide limestone stove with 2–3 fire holes): burns charcoal or wood. **Fanning it** (a palm or cane fan) makes it cook faster.
- **Il-borma** † (stew pot) on a fire hole:
  - Put ingredients in one at a time. **The pot shows what's in it.**
  - When the recipe is complete it simmers for a while, then **gives 4–6 servings** into bowls.
  - Soups and stews (aljotta, kawlata, minestra, stuffat tal-fenek, stuffat tal-qarnit…) are made this way.
- **It-taġen** † (frying pan): fried lampuka, fenek moqli, imqaret, żeppoli. Quick, one or two at a time.
- **The crafting table only prepares:** dough, pastry, zalza, ħwawar, mince. **Maltese dishes are cooked only on the kenur or in the forn** (owner).
- **Placed dishes:** a finished dish can be set on a table (a pot of stuffat, a plate of pastizzi, a ftira, a timpana, a tray of ġbejniet) and eaten from in turns.
- **The table bonus** (owner's rule: a reward, never a penalty). A meal eaten at a laid table, **with other players or villagers there**, or **made up of different kinds of food** (bread, beans, vegetables, fish or meat, cheese), gives **"Mitmugħ tajjeb" †** ("well fed"): a lasting bit of extra health and healing. This ties to the shared table and the fenkata in [[Maltese life ideas]] (72, 79).

**Art:**
- kenur (fire states), borma (a few fill and colour states), taġen, fan, ladle, plates;
- **a placed model for each placeable dish** (about 12): the biggest art job in the mod.

## 2. Il-Forn: bread

**Real Malta:**
- **Ħobż tal-Malti** is a sourdough of flour, starter, water and sea salt ✓. It takes **seven to eight hours** from kneading to the oven ✓ and is baked in **wood-fired ovens** ✓.
- Few homes had an oven, so **on Sundays families took their dish to the village baker** ✓.
- **Most windmills were built by the Knights between 1663 and 1773** to grind flour ✓. Ta' Kola still has its sails and machinery ✓.

**In the game:**
- **Il-forn** (a domed stone oven, 3×3 or so):
  - Fire it with wood. It **holds its heat** for a good while.
  - It **bakes in batches**: up to 12 loaves, ftajjar or pastizzi at once.
  - Bread, ftira, pastizzi, qassatat, the sweets, and the big oven dishes (timpana, ross il-forn, lampuki pie) are baked **only here**.
- **Il-ħmira** (sourdough starter) ✓: made once from flour and water, then kept in a jar.
  - Each bake takes a piece and you top it up.
  - **A piece can be given to a friend.** It's an ingredient, not a chore: it never dies.
- **Flour:**
  - **A hand quern** † (a small block) grinds wheat into flour.
  - **The windmill** (il-mitħna; a later phase, big art) grinds **more flour per wheat, plus bran** for animals. Its sails turn faster in a stronger wind (vanilla weather: faster in rain and storms). It's a bonus and a landmark, not a requirement.
- **The village baker** (Maltese life, idea 80): a villager with a forn who bakes your Sunday dish into a better version.

**Art:** forn (fire and glow states), loaves in the oven, starter jar, baker's peel, quern. Later: the windmill (tower, cap, **animated sails**).

## 3. Il-Kantina: the pantry

These blocks make the **preserved ingredients** the dishes need: salt, kunserva, capers, cured olives, anchovies, dried and peppered ġbejniet. There's no spoiling, so preserving is about **making ingredients**, not racing a timer.

**Real Malta:**
- The **Xwejni salt pans** ✓: sea water dries in rock-cut pans from about May to September ✓. The salt is **swept up on harvest days** ✓ and **stored in caves in the coastal rock** ✓.
- Tomatoes are salted and **sun-dried** ✓, and **kunserva** is matured in the sun ✓.
- Capers are **brined** for weeks ✓.
- **Ġbejniet** are made with sheep's milk, salt and rennet ✓, **drained in rush baskets (qwieleb)** ✓ and **dried in a cane cage hung in the kitchen** ✓. They come fresh, dried, or peppered and kept in oil or vinegar ✓.

**In the game:**
- **Salt pans** (il-melħa): cut shallow basins into coastal rock and fill them with sea water.
  - In sunshine a salt crust forms over a few days. Sweep it with a broom.
  - **Bonus:** pans in summer and in `salina_saltworks` work faster.
  - Rain only pauses them.
  - The salt is better than draft 1's cauldron salt, which stays as a slow, small way to make it.
- **Drying rack and roof tray:** lay out salted tomatoes, figs, peppers or fish in daylight and they dry. **Faster in full sun and in summer**; rain pauses it.
- **Jars** (2–3 sizes): capers in brine, cured olives, anchovies in salt, tuna in oil, peppered ġbejniet in oil. Each takes a set time.
  - **Bonus:** jars in a **cool cellar** (a room cut into rock or underground) are ready sooner.
- **Ġbejniet:**
  1. Milk sheep (milkable in the mod).
  2. Warm the milk in a borma, add salt and **rennet**: **lamb rennet (tames ✓) or a plant rennet †** (owner: both).
  3. Ladle the curds into **qwieleb**, rush baskets woven from the wetland rushes (simar).
  4. **Fresh ġbejniet** come out after a day.
  5. Hang them in the **cane cage** to dry. **Faster where air moves** (an open window, a roof).
  6. Dried ġbejniet + black pepper in a jar of oil = **peppered**.
  - The whey left in the pot makes **ricotta**.

**Art:** salt pan states, broom, salt sack, drying rack and tray (with contents), jars (with contents), qwieleb (empty, full), the cane cage (with cheeses showing).

## 4. L-Għalqa: the field (bonuses only)

**Real Malta:**
- Maltese farmland is **saqwi** (irrigated) or **bagħli** (rain-fed) ✓. Irrigated land is about 11% of farmland ✓ but yields **at least 3.5 times as much** ✓.
- Water came from **wells and cisterns** (bir, ġiebja ✓), lifted by a pulley frame (ġabja ✓) or the animal-turned water wheel (**sienja** ✓).
- Bees were kept in **clay jar hives (qolla)** ✓ and in **rock-cut apiaries (miġbħa)** ✓, which kept colonies cool in summer and warm in winter ✓. Honey follows the flowers: **thyme in summer, carob in autumn** ✓.

**In the game:**
- **Vanilla farming is untouched.** Farmland near water grows as it always has.
- **The saqwi bonus:**
  - Crops on farmland **watered from a Maltese source**, meaning a stone channel fed from a **well (bir)** or **cistern (ġiebja)**, grow **faster and give extra harvest**.
  - The **sienja** (a later, big build; animated wheel and donkey) feeds a long run of channels at once.
  - It's a reward for building the Maltese way, not a cost for not doing it.
- **The in-season bonus** (only if the calendar is on; see the questions): a crop or fruit tree **in its Maltese season** grows a bit faster and yields a bit more. For example, tomatoes in summer, broad beans in spring, olives in autumn. Out of season it grows like vanilla.
- **Maltese bees:**
  - **The qolla** (clay hive) works like a vanilla beehive, with a **zeida** ✓ pot added for more honey.
  - **In a miġbħa** (a stone wall with hive niches) it makes more honey all year.
  - **The honey type follows nearby flowers:** thyme honey (garrigue), carob honey (carob trees), sulla honey (fields). Each is a different ingredient.

**Art:** well-head and pulley frame, cistern mouth, stone channels, qolla (plain, with zeida), the miġbħa wall, three honeys. Later: the sienja.

## 5. Il-Magħsra: presses

**Real Malta:**
- **Roman olive presses** are found across Malta: stone crushers (**trapetum**), press beds, and the anchor stones of lever and screw presses ✓.
- The Żejtun villa was a major oil producer ✓. The **ancient olive trees at Bidnija** are among Malta's oldest ✓.
- Malta's own grapes are red **Ġellewża** and white **Girgentina** ✓.

**In the game:**
- **The olive press:**
  - A stone **crusher** (a round basin with a turning stone; by hand, or faster with a donkey) turns olives into paste.
  - A **screw press** turns the paste into oil in a jar.
  - **Bidni** olives from old trees give a richer oil †.
- **The grape press and barrels:**
  - Press grapes into must, and fill barrels.
  - The must turns into **wine** over time: Ġellewża (red) or Girgentina (white).
  - **Bonus:** barrels in a cool cellar make **better wine**.
- Oil and wine are ingredients for most Maltese dishes. Draft 1's simple crafting-table oil and wine stay as the small, early way. The presses give **much more** and better.

**Art:** crusher (animated stone), screw press, oil jar, grape press, barrels (states).

## 6. Il-Baħar: the sea

**Real Malta:**
- Maltese fishermen use **cane and wicker traps (nassa)** ✓:
  - the **nassa tal-arznell** of fine cane, for picarel, **used only on Gozo** ✓;
  - the **nassa tal-qarnit** for octopus ✓;
  - the **nassa tal-vopi** for bogue ✓.
- **Kannizzati** ✓ are palm-frond rafts anchored with limestone ✓. They draw lampuki from **15 August to the end of December** ✓, a method barely changed since Roman times ✓.

**In the game:**
- **Nassa** (woven from cane, the qasba): set one on the seabed with bait and come back later.
  - The kind of trap decides the catch: picarel, octopus or bogue.
  - Vanilla fishing is unchanged.
- **Kannizzati:**
  - A raft set offshore **in the lampuki season** gathers a shoal you can net.
  - Out of season, lampuki are rare.
- **Rod fishing** in the world's Maltese sea biomes (Posidonia meadows, rocky shore, deep water) can bring up the Maltese fish of that water.

**Art:** three nassa models (with catch showing), the kannizzata raft, a net.
